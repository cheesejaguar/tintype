# Combined bibliography for Tintype detection research

**376 records** across both reviews as of November 13, 2026. This includes academic papers, challenge reports, and relevant methods in statistics, efficient inference, and provenance. The [combined report](../../reports/Tintype%20detection%20improvement%20research.md) uses a subset of these studies for the ranked recommendations.

The original review contributed 267 numbered records from its [topic notes](#research-notes). The annotated review contributes 109 additional records after matching 87 entries by arXiv ID, proceedings URL, title, or named method and authors. An annotation on an original entry indicates that both reviews covered the same work. A newer preprint revision or proceedings version represents the same work when applicable. The original notes retain their evidence-depth tags. Key claims in the report link directly to the paper or archived Tintype result. The original spot checks and ID corrections are recorded in the [pre-consolidation bibliography](https://github.com/cheesejaguar/tintype/blob/dcbe7d6ba1389c15052c02b452659963f3f2dc59/research_notes/Tintype%20detection%20improvement%20research/99_bibliography.md#spot-check-results-targeted-prioritised-by-how-much-a-recommendation-leans-on-the-paper).

**Original reading tags.** `V` means numbers checked in the paper, `u` indicates the note's default primary-source check, and `fetched` indicates the primary page was opened. `A`, `R`, `abs`, and `rec` indicate abstract-level or recalled evidence, `search` and `snip` indicate limited search evidence, and `sec`, `ctx`, and `lead` indicate secondary, contextual, or unverified references. The reading tags record how the original notes used each work. Annotated entries summarize published or author-reported evidence in the cited protocol.

| 342 | [Reality Perception Gap: Are AI-Manipulated Video Detectors Ready for the Real World?](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14941v1) | Liu et al., Nov 2026 preprint | Ten thousand frontier full and partial synthesis clips with simulated repost compression and camera artifacts. None of 18 tested detectors exceeds 15% recall at 1% FPR. D3 ranks highest by AUC while WaveRep ranks highest at the 1% operating point. Positive controls and an encoding sweep implicate both generator/content shift and trace loss on repeated recompression. Authors did not report Tintype. |
| 343 | [CCDF: A Benchmark Dataset for Deepfake Detection in Surveillance Footage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07939v2) | Chopin et al., Oct 2026 preprint revision | 460 real, 1,380 synthetic clips from Grok Imagine, Veo 3.1, and Sora 2 across 16 categories. Ten detectors struggle, with raw, format-controlled, and altered variants supporting separate diagnosis. This is evidence for a controlled surveillance slice, with conclusions scoped to CCTV. |
| 344 | [DF26: We Cannot Tell Fake From Real Anymore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07369) | Shykula et al., Sep 2026 preprint | 271 real and 2,420 synthetic single-speaker videos across seven recent T2V and I2V models. Humans and tested detectors approach chance according to the authors. Useful for the talking-head slice, with one speaker per clip and a restricted content domain. |
| 345 | [SynthForensics: Benchmarking and Evaluating People-Centric Synthetic Video Deepfakes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04939v2) | Leotta et al., 2026 preprint v2, authors report NeurIPS | The expanded paper contains 20,445 videos from eight T2V and seven I2V open-source generators. Across 15 tested detectors, face-based methods lose 13–55 AUC points from FF++ to SynthForensics, and aggressive compression worsens scores. Fine-tuning to synthetic people harms some legacy detection. Use separately labeled face manipulation and fully synthetic people cohorts. |
| 346 | [Preserving Forgery Artifacts: AI-Generated Video Detection at Native Scale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04634) | Li et al., ICLR 2026 | A variable-resolution Qwen2.5-VL vision encoder retains high-frequency and spatiotemporal artifacts otherwise degraded by resizing and cropping. Supports a native-scale branch, with cost and training data jointly differing from smaller models. Test preserving native patches under a fixed Tintype crop budget. |
| 347 | [Training-free Detection of Text-to-video Generations via Over-coherence](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/papers/Brokman_Training-free_Detection_of_Text-to-video_Generations_via_Over-coherence_WACV_2026_paper.pdf) | Brokman et al., WACV 2026 | Combines extrema of adjacent-frame representation similarities and reports 0.838 and 0.783 AUC on aggregated protocols with different real sources. Static and motion-matched negatives are necessary for interpreting this cue, which the later motion shortcut audit found brittle under matched motion. |
| 348 | [SPLIT: Training-Free AI-Generated and Partially Edited Video Detection via Spatial Patch-Level Incoherence and Temporal Roughness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02886) | Hyun and Kim, 2026, authors report ECCV | Frozen patch tokens expose local temporal roughness and irregular neighboring patch motion. Paper reports best baseline-relative recall at 0.1% FPR on three data sets and emphasizes real-only threshold transfer. RPG found much weaker results when tested on heavy repost and mixed manipulation, so prototype as an additional narrow candidate. |
| 349 | [Detecting AI-Generated Videos with Spiking Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05895v2) | Jang et al., 2026 preprint revision | Combines frame-level residuals and latent temporal smoothness with sparsely activated temporal modeling. Reports 93.14% mean accuracy over ten unseen GenVideo generators. Test event selection or adjacent residual pooling before committing to specialized spiking inference. |
| 350 | [EA-Swin: An Embedding-Agnostic Swin Transformer for AI-Generated Video Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17260v2) | Mai et al., 2026 preprint revision | Designs factorized window attention over ViT-style video tokens and tests on a 130K-clip aggregate. Strong reported numbers apply to its training, curation and splits. Suggests an alternative if a much smaller temporal readout underfits. |
| 351 | [Beyond Pixels: Mining Compressed Domain Artifacts for Efficient AI-Generated Video Detection](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/zhu26ap.html) | Zhu et al., ICML 2026 | STREAM uses I-frames, motion vectors, and codec residual errors with gated fusion. Reports 0.965 mAP and 2.5× speed versus its baselines. Compare compressed-domain routing under matched codec/GOP controls and measure actual parsing plus fallback latency on the supported backends. |
| 352 | [Human Action CLIPs: Detecting AI-generated Human Motion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00526) | Bohacek and Farid, IJCAI workshop 2025 | Uses multimodal semantic features for matched human actions in DeepAction and reports robustness to tested compression and resolution laundering. Supports a human-motion transfer diagnostic distinct from face-only detection; evaluate gameplay and styled capture before using a motion rule. |
| 353 | [ReConFuse: Reconstruction-Error Guided Semantic Fusion for AI-Generated Video Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04706) | Chen et al., 2026 preprint | Combines WF-VAE reconstruction errors, frame semantics, and Mamba temporal processing. Stage-2 candidate or offline teacher only if it adds recall after conditioning on the fast score and transformed-domain quality. |
| 354 | [Revealing Artifacts via Noise Amplification: A Novel Perspective for AI-Generated Video Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16742) | Cheng et al., 2026 preprint | Amplifies low-bit-plane signals for a learned video discriminator and tests across generators. Low-cost YUV or luma residuals are plausible candidates for an independently trained branch. Recompute under both decoder backends, quality settings and color depth before using the feature. |
| 355 | [SAGA: Source Attribution of Generative AI Videos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12834) | Kundu et al., CVPR 2026 | Temporal attention signatures and hard negatives support attribution at multiple levels in its benchmark. Useful precedent for evaluating transfer across generator families and inspecting which families share fingerprints. Source attribution remains a separate outcome from binary detection. |
| 356 | [Benchmarking and Improving Perceptual Straightening for Robust AI-Generated Video Detection](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/15/15/3245) | Papougiannakis and Tous, Electronics 2026 | Fusing geometry and learned temporal scores helps on some data, yet on DeepAction an illustrative fusion shifts fake recall upward for two generators while real accuracy falls to 0.460 and AUC to 0.769. Inspect fusion at matched low FPR and across real sources. |
| 357 | [Explainable Forensics of Manipulated Segments in Untrimmed Long Videos](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/feng26g.html) | Feng et al., ICML 2026 | TASLE has 12,472 untrimmed videos and boundaries/rationales, with MSLoc using cheap proposals followed by more precise analysis. Supports window-level recall and timestamp outputs for brief insertions inside long real clips. |
| 358 | [ActivityForensics: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Localizing Manipulated Activity in Videos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03819) | Bao et al., CVPR 2026 | More than 6,000 activity-level forged segments, designed to blend into the video, with cross-domain and open-world protocols. Useful for testing later, brief clips where averaging all frame logits dilutes signal. |
| 359 | [Detecting Temporally Localized Manipulations in Authentic Video Streams](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07090) | Umur et al., 2026 preprint | Uses a DINOv3 linear probe and consecutive-frame similarity to identify those boundaries in authentic streams. Supports testing both abrupt boundary and within-segment cues on realistic insertions, with a limited author-curated test. |
| 360 | [FaceForensics++: Learning to Detect Manipulated Facial Images](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/html/Rossler_FaceForensics_Learning_to_Detect_Manipulated_Facial_Images_ICCV_2019_paper.html) | Rössler et al., ICCV 2019 | Four major face manipulation methods with compression controls. Suitable for a face-edit profile and for testing robustness; assumes facial manipulations and older generation pipelines. |
| 361 | [Beyond Spatial Frequency: Pixel-wise Temporal Frequency-based Deepfake Video Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02398v2) | Kim et al., ICCV 2025 | Applies a temporal FFT at each face pixel and learns an attention proposal to isolate motion-related evidence. An adjacent-frame frequency feature is a transfer hypothesis for generic video after controls for FPS, scene cuts and changes in motion. |
| 362 | [TalkingHeadBench: A Multi-Modal Benchmark & Analysis of Talking-Head DeepFake Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24866v3) | Xiong et al., WACV 2026 | Holds out identities and generation methods in talking heads made by modern academic and commercial models. Benchmark separate face/speech routes and evaluate subject coverage independently from scene synthesis. |
| 363 | [ForgeryNet: A Versatile Benchmark for Comprehensive Forgery Analysis](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/He_ForgeryNet_A_Versatile_Benchmark_for_Comprehensive_Forgery_Analysis_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf) | He et al., CVPR 2021 | Provides face manipulation annotations for temporal localization, spatial localization, image classification and video classification. Its separate task definitions transfer to benchmark design, while generator cues are face-specific. |
| 364 | [AV-Deepfake1M++: A Large-Scale Audio-Visual Deepfake Benchmark with Real-World Perturbations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20579) | Cai et al., ACM MM 2025 | Tests localization and robustness of temporally sparse audio/visual tracks under dissemination perturbations. Separate visual and audio labels and require visual ground truth when assessing the visual-only Tintype detector. |
| 365 | [Frame Selection Strategies for Video Deepfake Detection: Benchmarking Accuracy and Runtime Trade-Offs](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/11/5364) | Serackis et al., Applied Sciences 2026 | Across 12 selection strategies and four face detectors, 32 selected frames led average discrimination on the 300-video factorial test, but the mean AUC gap to 8 frames was 0.0069. The best sampler varied by detector and uniform sampling was the cheapest. The 1,180-clip validation offers fewer high-frame-budget comparisons. Use as face-specific support for measuring strategy and runtime jointly. |
| 366 | [AEGIS: Authenticity Evaluation Benchmark for AI-Generated Video Sequences](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10771) | Li et al., 2025 preprint | Curates more than 10,000 clips across open and commercial generators with hard subsets and annotations for semantic, motion, and low-level evidence. Evaluated VLMs struggled on the hard subsets; compare their predictions to spatial and temporal baselines under the same preprocessing. |
| 367 | [MARS: Multiscale Appearance-Residual Streams for Video Forgery Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12508) | Radwan and Raza, November 2026 preprint | A gated detector combines an appearance branch and fixed differences of intermediate features across spatial and temporal scales. Reports transfer from face manipulation training to full synthesis, with 93.99% AUROC on GenVideo and 75.50% on DF26. Test a small residual head against equal-budget additional frame views on real-source and codec-matched data before extrapolating. |
