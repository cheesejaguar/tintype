# Low-level, frequency and artifact-based detection of AI-generated images: what could raise Tintype's recall, precision or speed

Scope: the literature on spectral, upsampling-artifact, noise-residual and local-pixel-correlation detectors (2019–2026), reconstruction and zero-shot detectors, robustness to JPEG and resizing, two-stream fusion, and native-resolution patch selection. Each paper gets a "Tintype applicability" note. The Tintype baseline is a ViT-S/16 at 224 px from Community Forensics, with six views (one fit view, a centre crop and four most-textured native crops) and early exit. Its weak spots are messenger copies (27–36% recall), NTIRE-2026 distorted inputs (2.2% recall at 1% FPR; 155/600 real distorted inputs flagged), GANs (84–89%) and face swaps (14%). docs/RESEARCH.md already covers CNNSpot, NPR, PatchCraft, SAFE, FreDect, LGrad, UnivFD, AIDE, DRCT, B-Free and Community Forensics at the family level. These notes add per-paper mechanisms, numbers and the 2025–26 evidence.

Verification legend. **[V]** means the numbers were read from the paper (arXiv abstract or HTML full text) during this session. **[A]** means the citation and core idea are well established but the figures come from the paper's abstract as I recall it and were not re-fetched this session. The report writer should treat [A] figures as needing a spot check.

## Q1. Which spectral, upsampling, noise-residual and local-correlation signals generalise across GAN, diffusion and autoregressive generators?

### Takeaway
Hand-crafted low-level cues (spectral peaks, up-sampling correlations as in NPR, gradients as in LGrad, rich/poor texture contrast as in PatchCraft) generalise well *within* the CNN-upsampler world: GANs and early pixel-space or LDM diffusion. They collapse on 2024–26 commercial generators. In independent 2025 benchmarks NPR scores 42.7–46.1% balanced accuracy, i.e. at or below chance, and AIDE and SAFE score 47–54%. The low-level ideas that do generalise in 2025–26 are not hand-crafted features. They are training-protocol ideas: crop rather than resize, keep native resolution, model the real-image spectrum (SPAI), and remove format bias. Each is used to train a general backbone.

### Cited Findings

**Classic spectral and upsampling papers (2019–2020)**

- **Zhang, Karaman & Chang, "Detecting and Simulating Artifacts in GAN Fake Images" (AutoGAN), WIFS 2019.** Shows that the up-sampling (transposed-conv) layers shared by GANs create periodic spectral replicas. A classifier on the *spectrum* instead of pixels generalises across GAN architectures, and the artifact can be simulated with a GAN-free "AutoGAN" pipeline, so no GAN training data is needed. [A] — [arXiv 1907.06515](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06515)
  - *Tintype applicability:* simulating up-sampling artifacts on real photos (for example, passing a real crop through a random learned or transposed-conv up-sampler) is a cheap source of extra "fake" positives aimed at the GAN weak spot (84–89% recall). Low cost, since it is a data-pipeline change. Risk: the artifact is a high-frequency periodic pattern that JPEG at Q≤75 and downscaling largely erase, so it will not help messenger copies.
- **Frank et al., "Leveraging Frequency Analysis for Deep Fake Image Recognition," ICML 2020.** In the 2D-DCT domain, GAN images show grid-like high-frequency artifacts caused by up-sampling. A linear model on log-DCT coefficients rivals CNNs on GAN detection. [A] — [arXiv 2003.08685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08685)
  - *Tintype applicability:* a log-DCT feature vector is a near-free auxiliary input (one DCT on a 224 crop). It is plausibly useful only as a GAN-specialist head. Expected effect: small recall gain on GAN sets for unprocessed files, none on messenger copies. Risk: DCT statistics are dominated by JPEG quantisation on recompressed images, which raises false-positive risk on heavily compressed real photos.
- **Durall, Keuper & Keuper, "Watch your Up-Convolution: CNN Based Generative Deep Neural Networks are Failing to Reproduce Spectral Distributions," CVPR 2020.** Up-convolutions make GANs fail to reproduce the high-frequency fall-off of the azimuthally averaged power spectrum. A 1D spectrum profile plus a simple classifier detects them, and a spectral loss during GAN training removes the cue. [A] — [arXiv 2003.01826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.01826)
  - *Tintype applicability:* the 1D radial-spectrum profile is a cheap diagnostic, and the paper shows it can be *trained away* by a generator. Do not rely on it as a primary feature. It is useful for analysing what the ViT misses on GAN sets.
- **Wang et al., "CNN-generated images are surprisingly easy to spot… for now" (CNNSpot), CVPR 2020.** A ResNet-50 trained on ProGAN only, with blur and JPEG augmentation, generalises to other CNN generators. Augmentation was key to robustness. [A] — [arXiv 1912.11035](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.11035). Its generalisation to modern generators is poor (37.5% mean accuracy in the 2026 out-of-the-box benchmark already cited in docs/RESEARCH.md).
  - *Tintype applicability:* nothing new beyond the augmentation principle, which Tintype already uses.

**Diffusion-era fingerprints (2022–2023)**

- **Corvi et al., "On the detection of synthetic images generated by diffusion models," ICASSP 2023.** Diffusion images carry spectral peaks in the noise residual. Detectors trained on GANs transfer poorly to diffusion, and vice versa. Training on LDM images with heavy augmentation transfers better, and robustness to resizing and compression drops sharply. [A] — [arXiv 2211.00680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00680)
- **Corvi et al., "Intriguing properties of synthetic images: from GANs to diffusion," CVPRW 2023.** The autocorrelation and power spectra of noise residuals (denoiser output subtracted) show generator-specific periodic peaks. These are strong for GANs and weaker but present for LDMs. Modern generators' mid/low-frequency statistics also differ from real photos. [A] — [arXiv 2304.06408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06408)
  - *Tintype applicability for both Corvi papers:* (a) a residual-autocorrelation visualisation is a good *diagnostic* for which of Tintype's misses on old GANs keep their periodic fingerprint. (b) If the fingerprint survives, a residual-input auxiliary view becomes worth considering. Cost: low for diagnostics. Risk: residual peaks vanish after resizing.
- **Ricker, Damm, Holz & Fischer, "Towards the Detection of Diffusion Model Deepfakes," VISAPP 2024.** Diffusion models show far weaker frequency artifacts than GANs. Detectors trained on GANs fail on diffusion models, and retraining on diffusion output is required. [A] — [arXiv 2210.14571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14571)
- **Bammey, "Synthbuster: Towards Detection of Diffusion Model Generated Images," IEEE Open Journal of Signal Processing, 2023.** Builds the RAISE-based uncompressed benchmark (9 diffusion generators vs. RAW-derived reals) and a spectral-residual detector. It shows that the periodic spectral traces of diffusion models are detectable on clean images but not robust. [A] — [DOI 10.1109/OJSP.2023.3337714](https://doi.org/10.1109/OJSP.2023.3337714)
  - *Tintype applicability:* Synthbuster is already a Tintype benchmark. The detector is not worth porting.

**Up-sampling, gradient, frequency CNNs (2021–2024; Tan et al. line and relatives)**

- **Liu et al., "Spatial-Phase Shallow Learning: Rethinking Face Forgery Detection in Frequency Domain" (SPSL), CVPR 2021.** The *phase* spectrum is sensitive to up-sampling and generalises better across face-forgery methods than amplitude. It uses shallow networks on phase plus RGB. [A] — [arXiv 2103.01412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01412)
  - *Tintype applicability:* relevant to the **face-swap** weak spot (14%). Face swaps blend a generated face (up-sampled) into a real frame, and phase-based cues target exactly that blend boundary. A phase-spectrum auxiliary head on a face crop is a possible add-on, at medium cost because it needs a face detector and face-crop view. Risk: SPSL was evaluated on FaceForensics++ compression levels, where performance falls at c40 (heavy compression).
- **Jeong et al., "BiHPF: Bilateral High-Pass Filters for Robust Deepfake Detection," WACV 2022.** Amplifies frequency-level artifacts with a learnable high-pass filter in both frequency and pixel space. It improved GAN generalisation when trained on few GAN classes. [A] — CVF WACV 2022, [openaccess](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2022/html/Jeong_BiHPF_Bilateral_High-Pass_Filters_for_Robust_Deepfake_Detection_WACV_2022_paper.html)
  - *Tintype applicability:* low. It is superseded by NPR and FreqNet, and its high-pass emphasis is exactly what JPEG removes.
- **Tan et al., "Learning on Gradients: Generalized Artifacts Representation for GAN-Generated Images Detection" (LGrad), CVPR 2023.** Uses gradients of a pretrained CNN with respect to the input as a generator-agnostic artifact map, then classifies those maps. The abstract claims about +11.4% mean accuracy over prior SOTA on GANs. [A] — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Tan_Learning_on_Gradients_Generalized_Artifacts_Representation_for_GAN-Generated_Images_Detection_CVPR_2023_paper.html). On 2025 generators LGrad scores 54.4% bAcc on B-Free's Synthbuster/new-generator/WildRF suite and 65.9% AUC on SPAI's 13-generator suite. [V] — [B-Free HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17671v2); [SPAI HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19417v2)
  - *Tintype applicability:* not worth it. It needs a second CNN backward pass per view (at least 2× cost) and does not generalise to modern generators.
- **Tan et al., "Frequency-Aware Deepfake Detection: Improving Generalizability through Frequency Space Learning" (FreqNet), AAAI 2024.** A 1.9M-parameter CNN that applies high-frequency representation and convolution in the frequency domain (phase and amplitude) at several layers. The abstract claims +9.8% over SOTA with far fewer parameters, on 17 GANs and diffusion models. [A] — [arXiv 2403.07240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07240)
  - *Tintype applicability:* interesting only as a **tiny GAN specialist** (1.9M parameters, roughly one tenth of ViT-S) that could run as a cheap second opinion on uncertain images. That would be faster than invoking the 384-px Community Forensics network. Risk: like all high-frequency CNNs, it is likely fragile on messenger copies.
- **Tan et al., "Rethinking the Up-Sampling Operations in CNN-based Generative Network for Generalizable Deepfake Detection" (NPR), CVPR 2024.** Neighbouring Pixel Relationships: subtracting a 2×-nearest-down-up-sampled copy from the image exposes the local correlation left by up-sampling. A small ResNet trained on 4-class ProGAN reports +11.6% over previous methods across 28 generators. [A] — [arXiv 2312.10461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10461). Independent 2025 evaluations are far worse: 42.7% average bAcc on Synthbuster, new generators and WildRF ([B-Free](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17671v2)), 45.4% average AUC on SPAI's 13 generators ([SPAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19417v2)), and 46.1% average / 2.9% minimum accuracy over 11 benchmarks ([DDA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14359v6)). [V]
  - *Tintype applicability:* adding an NPR residual as an extra input channel or view is cheap (one subtraction per crop). The evidence above, however, says NPR's signal is either already captured or not useful on current generators, and that it does not survive real-world processing. Do not prioritise it. A narrow use remains possible: an NPR view only for images that show no sign of recompression, aimed at GANs.

**Local pixel correlation and texture contrast (2023–2025)**

- **Zhong et al., "PatchCraft: Exploring Texture Patch for Efficient AI-generated Image Detection" (earlier title "Rich and Poor Texture Contrast…"), arXiv 2023.** The "Smash & Reconstruct" step cuts an image into patches, ranks them by texture richness, and reassembles a rich-texture image and a poor-texture image. A detector then learns the *contrast* in inter-pixel correlation between them, using 30 SRM-style high-pass filters. The premise is that generators reproduce rich textures less faithfully than flat ones. [A] — [arXiv 2311.12397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12397). Independent: 80.4% average AUC on SPAI's 13-generator set, third best there. [V] — [SPAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19417v2)
  - *Tintype applicability:* the texture-ranking idea is already in Tintype's view selection. The new element is the *rich-versus-poor contrast*, i.e. also looking at a flat patch. It is cheap to try as a seventh view (the least-textured crop). Expected effect: unclear, and possibly a small recall gain. Risk: flat patches in JPEG copies are dominated by 8×8 block artifacts, which raises false positives on compressed real photos.
- **Li et al., "Improving Synthetic Image Detection Towards Generalization: An Image Transformation Perspective" (SAFE), KDD 2025.** Three training changes: crop instead of resize, ColorJitter plus RandomRotation, and patch-based random masking, motivated by the stronger local pixel correlation of synthetic images. Reported gains over SOTA are +4.5% accuracy and +2.9% AP on an open-world set from 26 generators. [V] — [arXiv 2408.06741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06741). Independent: 47.6% average / 1.1% minimum accuracy on DDA's 11 benchmarks, and SAFE's score on VAE reconstructions "dropped sharply when high frequencies were slightly masked." [V] — [DDA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14359v6)
  - *Tintype applicability:* Tintype already crops at native resolution. **Patch-based random masking** is a cheap augmentation to add. Avoid ColorJitter: SPAI found that chromatic augmentations cut its AUC from 91.0 to 80.5. [V] — [SPAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19417v2). Risk: SAFE's own collapse shows how fragile models are that ride high-frequency local correlation.
- **Yan et al., "A Sanity Check for AI-generated Image Detection" (AIDE + Chameleon dataset), ICLR 2025.** A hybrid detector with two streams. The low-level stream takes the highest- and lowest-frequency patches (by DCT score) and passes them through SRM filters and a ResNet. The semantic stream is CLIP. Reported gains are +3.5% and +4.6% on AIGCDetectBenchmark and GenImage. The Chameleon set showed that nine off-the-shelf detectors called almost all its AI images real. [V] — [arXiv 2406.19435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19435). Independent: 53.2% bAcc on B-Free's suite, 64.0% average on GenImage/FakeInversion/SynthWildX ([B-Free](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17671v2)), and 54.1% average / 19.1% minimum on DDA's 11 benchmarks ([DDA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14359v6)). [V]
  - *Tintype applicability:* the evidence against bolting a hand-crafted SRM/DCT stream onto a semantic backbone trained on narrow data is strong. AIDE's gains did not transfer. Tintype's broad-data end-to-end ViT is the better recipe. AIDE's *highest-and-lowest* patch choice is a cheap view-ordering idea (see Q5).

**Spectral learning that does generalise (2025)**

- **Karageorgiou, Papadopoulos, Kompatsiaris & Gavves, "Any-Resolution AI-Generated Image Detection by Spectral Learning" (SPAI), CVPR 2025.** [V] — [arXiv 2411.19417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19417), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19417v2)
  - Method: a frozen ViT-B/16 pretrained with *masked frequency modelling* on real images only. The FFT is split by a circular mask of radius 16 into low-pass and high-pass versions. "Spectral reconstruction similarity" (cosine similarity between the original, low-pass and high-pass token embeddings at every block, 72 statistics) measures how out-of-distribution an image's spectrum is relative to real images. "Spectral context attention" aggregates any number of 224×224 patches at O(K) cost, so it handles 1000-MP images.
  - Trained on 180k real plus 180k images from one LDM, at 256 px.
  - Results: 91.0 average AUC over 13 generators (Glide through Firefly, FLUX, SD3, MJ v6.1, DALL·E 3), against 85.5 for RINE, 80.4 PatchCraft, 65.9 LGrad, 50.8 DIRE and 45.4 NPR. Weakest generators: SD3 at 75.9 and FLUX at 83.0. Balanced accuracy is 82.3 at the 0.5 threshold.
  - Ablations: without spectral pretraining, 52.5. Replacing SCA with a centre crop gives 83.2, ten-crop mean 85.3 and ten-crop max 84.2. Without JPEG augmentation, 89.1. Without noise and blur, 84.2.
  - Robustness (JPEG/WebP at Q 85/70/50, blur, noise, resize to 85/70/50%): the paper reports beating its two strongest competitors in every case, but exact values are only in a figure.
  - Stated limitations: screenshots, memes and recaptures defeat it, and "compression and noisy channels can erase the subtle spectral cues."
  - *Tintype applicability:* two transferable ideas. (1) **Aggregating patches with learned attention instead of mean logits.** SPAI's own ablation shows that its attention over all patches beats ten-crop mean by about 5.7 AUC. For Tintype this means a small attention-pooling head over the per-view CLS tokens of its 6 views. Cost: a small head plus a retrain. Speed: neutral at fixed view count, but it conflicts with early exit unless pooling is done incrementally. (2) **Spectral pretraining on real images** gives a "real-spectrum" prior that does not depend on any generator. Folding that into a ViT-S would mean a costly re-pretraining. As a second-stage model it costs a ViT-B forward pass plus two filtered passes, roughly 3× a ViT-B per patch, which is heavier than Tintype's whole pipeline. Risk: the authors themselves flag compression fragility, so it will not fix the messenger gap.

### Inferences
- Across the 2025 independent tables (B-Free, SPAI, DDA), every method built on a *hand-crafted* low-level representation (NPR, LGrad, CNNSpot, AIDE's SRM stream, SAFE) falls to about 42–65% on 2024–26 generators. The methods that hold up (B-Free 96.3 bAcc, DDA 90.7%, SPAI 91.0 AUC) are general ViT backbones trained with a low-level-aware *protocol*: native-resolution crops, aligned real/fake pairs, a real-spectrum prior. This supports Tintype's current design (plain ViT, native crops, broad data) over adding a hand-crafted stream.
- GAN fingerprints (periodic up-sampling peaks) are the most robust "classic" signal and are explicitly what Community Forensics' huge GAN coverage captures. Tintype's GAN gap is more likely a data-coverage and fine-tuning-drift issue (catastrophic forgetting of the CF checkpoint's GAN knowledge during 4-phase retraining) than a missing feature. A cheap fix is to replay GAN data, or to distil from the CF 384 model on GAN sets, rather than to add an NPR or FreqNet stream.
- For autoregressive and "FLUX-era" generators, no paper in this survey shows a hand-crafted spectral cue that generalises. SPAI's weakest results are on FLUX (83.0) and SD3 (75.9), the two newest architectures in its test set.

### Gaps
- No paper found that evaluates NPR, PatchCraft or SPAI specifically on GPT-image-1/2, Nano Banana or FLUX.2 outputs. Evidence on "autoregressive" generators comes only indirectly from DDA's EvalGEN (DDA 97.2%, DRCT 77.8%) [V — [DDA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14359v6)], which uses the authors' own benchmark.
- Exact JPEG-Q curves for SPAI are only in a figure that could not be extracted.

## Q2. Reconstruction, inversion and zero-shot methods: which are cheap enough for a fast detector or a second stage?

### Takeaway
Per-image diffusion inversion (DIRE, and to a lesser degree DRCT and FIRE at test time) is far too slow for Tintype's 77–560 images/s. It is also confounded by JPEG format bias. The lasting contribution of the reconstruction line is a **training-data trick, not a test-time detector.** VAE or self-conditioned reconstructions of *real* photos, used as fake training samples (DRCT, AlignedForensics, B-Free, DDA), force the detector onto generation artifacts rather than content or format. They add zero inference cost. Among zero-shot methods, only feature-perturbation tests in the style of RIGID (two forward passes of an existing encoder) are cheap enough for a second stage. Even those are best used as an extra feature or an abstain signal, not as a primary detector.

### Cited Findings

**Test-time reconstruction detectors**

- **Wang et al., "DIRE for Diffusion-Generated Image Detection," ICCV 2023.** Inverts the image with DDIM, reconstructs it, and classifies |x − recon| with a ResNet. It requires a full diffusion inversion per image, i.e. tens of U-Net evaluations. [A] — [arXiv 2303.09295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09295). Independent results on modern generators: 50.8% AUC on SPAI's 13-generator set. [V] — [SPAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19417v2). Grommelt et al. showed that GenImage-style datasets carry JPEG-format and size biases (reals are JPEG, fakes are PNG) that detectors exploit. [A] — ["Fake or JPEG? Revealing Common Biases in Generated Image Detection Datasets," arXiv 2403.17608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17608)
  - *Tintype applicability:* none at test time, because it is hundreds of times slower. The JPEG-bias lesson matters for Tintype's training mix (see Q3).
- **Ricker, Lukovnikov & Fischer, "AEROBLADE: Training-Free Detection of Latent Diffusion Images Using Autoencoder Reconstruction Error," CVPR 2024.** Passes the image through the LDM's VAE only (no diffusion) and scores the LPIPS distance between image and reconstruction. Images generated by that VAE reconstruct almost perfectly. The abstract reports average precision of about 0.99 on LDM generators. [A] — [arXiv 2401.17879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17879). It needs a VAE matching the generator and degrades under JPEG; the RIGID paper treats it as the prior training-free reference. [V] — [RIGID](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.20112)
  - *Tintype applicability:* the cost is moderate: one SD/FLUX-VAE encode and decode at native resolution is far heavier than a ViT-S/224 view. Using it as a second stage only on uncertain images with no sign of recompression is possible, but it covers only LDM families whose VAE you ship. GPT-image and other autoregressive models are not covered. Low priority.
- **Luo et al., "LaRE^2: Latent Reconstruction Error Based Method for Diffusion-Generated Image Detection," CVPR 2024.** Computes the reconstruction error in latent space with a single denoising step instead of a full inversion. That makes it much cheaper than DIRE, and it uses the error map to guide the image features. [A] — [arXiv 2403.17465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17465)
  - *Tintype applicability:* still needs a diffusion U-Net or DiT forward pass per image, far too heavy for the default path.
- **Chen et al., "DRCT: Diffusion Reconstruction Contrastive Training towards Universal Detection of Diffusion Generated Images," ICML 2024.** Builds hard negatives by *reconstructing real images with a diffusion model*, which yields DRCT-2M, and trains with contrastive loss so the detector separates real from its own reconstruction. [A] — [PMLR v235](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/chen24ay.html). Independent: 70.1% average / 50.6% minimum over DDA's 11 benchmarks, 77.8% on EvalGEN. [V] — [DDA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14359v6). The 2026 out-of-the-box benchmark already in docs/RESEARCH.md notes that DRCT variants swing 20–60 points depending on training set.
  - *Tintype applicability:* the *training-time* idea is what matters (see the alignment papers below). No test-time cost.
- **Chu et al., "FIRE: Robust Detection of Diffusion-Generated Images via Frequency-Guided Reconstruction Error," CVPR 2025.** Observes that diffusion models reconstruct mid-band frequencies of real images poorly. It learns a frequency mask and uses the frequency-guided reconstruction error, and claims better robustness than DIRE-style errors. [A] — [arXiv 2412.07140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07140)
  - *Tintype applicability:* still needs a diffusion forward and backward per image. The "mid-band" observation is consistent with SPAI's choice of a radius-16 low/high split. Low priority.

**Zero-shot or training-free detectors**

- **Cozzolino, Poggi, Nießner & Verdoliva, "Zero-Shot Detection of AI-Generated Images" (ZED), ECCV 2024.** A lossless neural image coder trained on *real images only* predicts each pixel's distribution from multi-resolution context. AI images are "less surprising" (lower coding cost relative to the expected value). The single feature needs no fake data. The abstract claims more than +3% average accuracy over SOTA. [V] — [arXiv 2409.15875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15875)
  - *Tintype applicability:* attractive in principle as an orthogonal second-stage signal that will not age as generators change. The coder is small, and multi-resolution context makes it reasonably fast. Coding cost, however, is a pixel-level statistic that JPEG requantisation changes directly, so expect it to fail on messenger copies. The abstract does not report compression robustness (Gap). Medium cost to port into Rust.
- **Brokman et al., "Manifold Induced Biases for Zero-shot and Few-shot Detection of Generated Images," ICLR 2025.** Derives a zero-shot criterion from the score function and curvature of a pretrained diffusion model's learned manifold: generated images sit in regions with a characteristic local manifold bias. It needs several diffusion-model evaluations per image. [A] — [OpenReview, ICLR 2025](https://openreview.net/forum?id=7gGl6HB5Zd)
  - *Tintype applicability:* too slow because it needs a diffusion backbone. Not recommended.
- **He, Chen & Ho, "RIGID: A Training-free and Model-Agnostic Framework for Robust AI-Generated Image Detection," arXiv 2024.** Adds small Gaussian noise to the image and measures cosine similarity between DINOv2 embeddings of the clean and perturbed copies. Real images give more stable representations. The abstract reports that average performance exceeds the best previous training-free method by more than 25%, and the authors describe it as robust to common corruptions. [V] — [arXiv 2405.20112](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.20112)
  - *Tintype applicability:* **the cheapest zero-shot idea for Tintype.** It could reuse Tintype's own ViT-S, or the CF backbone it already runs. Cost: one extra forward pass on a noised copy of the views already computed. Uses: (a) an extra scalar feature fed into the score fusion of the photo second opinion, or (b) an abstain or uncertainty signal. Expected effect: small, and unproven on a fine-tuned detector backbone, because RIGID's premise is a *generic* self-supervised encoder. Risk: noise sensitivity interacts with image noise level, and NTIRE-style noise and ISO distortions could flip it. Calibrate it on Tintype's real-photo sets before use.
- **WePe (training-free detection by weight perturbation).** The same family as RIGID, but it perturbs the encoder weights rather than the image. Not verified this session (see Gaps).

**Reconstruction as training-data alignment (the part worth adopting)**

- **Rajan, Ojha, Schloesser & Lee, "On the Effectiveness of Dataset Alignment for Fake Image Detection" (AlignedForensics), ICLR 2025.** Uses LDM-autoencoder reconstructions of real images as the fake class, so fakes and reals match in content, resolution and format. This forces the detector onto the VAE decoder's artifacts and makes dataset construction cheap. [A] — [arXiv 2410.11835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11835). Independent: the second-best method on DDA's 11-benchmark table at 75.0% average / 53.9% minimum. [V] — [DDA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14359v6)
- **Guillaro et al., "A Bias-Free Training Paradigm for More General AI-generated Image Detection" (B-Free), CVPR 2025.** [V] — [arXiv 2412.17671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17671), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17671v2)
  - Data: 51,517 COCO reals, each turned into six fakes with SD 2.1 *inpainting* using an empty mask (self-conditioned reconstruction) plus local inpainted variants, for 309,102 fakes in total.
  - Model: DINOv2-with-registers fine-tuned end to end at 504×504 crops, with no resizing in training or inference. Large images are tiled and logits averaged.
  - Results: 96.3 average bAcc on Synthbuster, new generators and WildRF, a +20.7-point margin over the runner-up. FLUX 92.3, SD3.5 98.9, Facebook 95.6, Reddit 86.2, Twitter 97.3. GenImage 89.3, FakeInversion 86.2, SynthWildX 95.6.
  - Ablation: reconstruction-only fakes give 80.7, adding inpainted fakes gives 92.2, and adding the ++ augmentations (scaling, cut-out, noise, jitter) gives 96.4. Linear probing gives 68.5 against 95.2 for full fine-tuning. Training a CLIP ViT on B-Free data reaches 73.9 against 45.2 on ProGAN data.
  - Robustness: under a social-network simulation on FakeInversion (resize 0.7–1, JPEG Q70–100), bAcc goes from 86.3 to 86.4 and AUC from 99.3 to 98.5. Above 80% bAcc in the hardest resize/JPEG/blur case.
  - Cost: data generation took 258.8 GPU-h according to DDA. [V]
  - *Tintype applicability:* see Q3. Two parts are relevant. **Inpainted fakes** directly address the "local inpainting out of scope" limit, and possibly face swaps, because both are local edits. **Self-conditioned reconstructions of Tintype's own real photo sources** act as content-aligned hard positives. No inference cost. Expected effect: higher recall on unseen generators and on local edits, and higher precision on content-confounded reals. Risk: the detector may start flagging real photos that went through a neural codec or AI enhancement (DDA flags smartphone AI enhancement as a limit, and NTIRE includes JPEG AI and Cheng2020 neural compression). This must be checked on NTIRE reals.
- **Chen et al., "Dual Data Alignment Makes AI-Generated Image Detector Easier Generalizable" (DDA), NeurIPS 2025.** [V] — [arXiv 2505.14359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14359), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14359v6)
  - Key observation: VAE reconstructions carry *more high-frequency energy* than JPEG-compressed real photos, so detectors trained on reconstruction pairs learn "high-frequency = fake." SAFE identified VAE reconstructions at 93% but collapsed when high frequencies were slightly masked.
  - Recipe: (1) SD-2.1-VAE reconstruction of real images. (2) *Frequency alignment*: estimate each real image's JPEG quality and re-encode its reconstruction at that quality, with probability 0.5. (3) Pixel mixup between real and reconstruction, with r drawn from U(0, R) and R in 0.2–0.8.
  - Model: DINOv2 ViT-L/14 with LoRA at 336 px.
  - Results: 90.7% average across 11 benchmarks, +15.7 over AlignedForensics, with an 81.4% minimum. Chameleon 82.4, Synthbuster 90.1, WildRF 90.3, EvalGEN 97.2, GenImage 91.7.
  - Weak spot: ForenSynths (GANs) at 81.4, below FatFormer's 90.1 and C2P-CLIP's 92.1.
  - Robustness: on GenImage-JPEG96 it leads the runner-up by +10.5 at JPEG 60, +4.1 at resize 2.0 and +5.7 at blur 2.0.
  - Cost: data construction takes 5.9 h, against 258.8 h for B-Free.
  - *Tintype applicability:* see Q3. This is the most directly relevant paper to Tintype's NTIRE false positives and messenger misses.

### Inferences
- For a Rust detector at about 2 ViT-S views per image, any test-time method that needs a diffusion U-Net, DiT or VAE pass costs one to three orders of magnitude more compute and is out of scope for the default path. The only candidates for a second stage are those reusing an existing ViT (RIGID-style perturbation consistency) or a small learned coder (ZED).
- The reconstruction literature's main practical value for Tintype is *training data*. VAE/self-conditioned reconstructions and inpainted variants of Tintype's own real photos, with JPEG quality matched as in DDA, give content-aligned fakes for free at inference time. DDA's 5.9-hour construction cost makes this affordable.
- DDA's finding (reconstruction fakes carry extra high frequencies) predicts that naively adding VAE reconstructions would *worsen* NTIRE behaviour, because the model would learn "sharp = fake." The JPEG-matching step is not optional.

### Gaps
- WePe (weight-perturbation training-free detection) was not verified this session, and no citation is given.
- No JPEG or resize robustness numbers were found for ZED, AEROBLADE or Manifold-bias. RIGID's robustness claim is from the paper's framing and summaries, not from a table read this session.
- No paper tests reconstruction-aligned training against neural-codec-compressed real photos (JPEG AI, Cheng2020), which is exactly the NTIRE condition.

## Q3. Which low-level signals survive JPEG and resizing, and which collapse?

### Takeaway
Explicit high-frequency cues (spectral peaks, NPR correlations, SRM residuals, VAE high-frequency energy) collapse under JPEG at Q≤75 and under downscaling. Many detectors' "low-level" signal is actually **format bias**: reals are JPEG, fakes are PNG, so the detector learns "no JPEG grid or extra high frequency = fake." That bias is exactly what produces both (a) false positives on clean or distorted real images and (b) misses on recompressed fakes. Methods that survive do so by *aligning* the classes in format and frequency (DDA, B-Free) or by heavy, symmetric distortion augmentation on a large foundation backbone (NTIRE 2026 winners), not by a more robust hand-crafted feature.

### Cited Findings
- **NTIRE 2026 Challenge on Robust AI-Generated Image Detection in the Wild** (Gushchin et al., CVPRW 2026). [V] — [arXiv 2604.11487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11487), [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/NTIRE/html/Gushchin_NTIRE_2026_Challenge_on_Robust_AI-Generated_Image_Detection_in_the_CVPRW_2026_paper.html)
  - Data: 108,750 real and 185,750 generated images from 42 generators. 36 distortions are applied in chains of 1–5 at several levels. They span blur (Gaussian, lens, motion, glass), noise (white, impulse, shot, ISO, multiplicative), compression (JPEG, multiple JPEG, JPEG 2000, **neural JPEG AI and Cheng2020**, and mixtures), colour/tone (shift, saturation, jitter, quantisation, RGB shift, brightness, contrast, tone curve, CLAHE), geometry (random and aspect crop, downscale, pixelation, perspective) and watermarking (invisible and adversarial watermarks).
  - Top results: robust AUC 0.9723 and 0.9721 against clean 0.9974 and 0.9972. Rank 1 (MICV) used committees of DINOv3 fine-tuned end to end at 512 px with a difficulty-aware augmentation pipeline. Rank 2 (Ant International) used two fine-tuned DINOv3-7B experts at 512 and 288 px, with four offline augmentation levels from clean to heavy.
  - The other top teams: rank 3 EVA-CLIP with LoRA trained on *paired clean/distorted* images, rank 4 a DINOv3-H and MetaCLIP2 ensemble at 256/448/378, rank 5 SigLIP2-Giant at 384 with distortions applied to every training image.
  - **None of the top six used an explicit frequency or noise-residual stream.** Only a lower-ranked team (RAPID) used an SRM branch. Organisers note that larger models and higher resolution helped consistently, and that robustness separated teams, with clean AUC about 0.98–0.99 against robust about 0.91–0.93 for mid-ranked teams.
  - *Tintype applicability:* Tintype's 2.2% recall at 1% FPR on NTIRE, and 155/600 flagged reals, point to a training-distribution gap, not a missing feature. Tintype trains with JPEG, resize kernels and messenger round trips, but none of NTIRE's noise, blur, colour, tone, JPEG 2000, neural-codec, pixelation or watermark operations. Adding an NTIRE-style chain (1–5 random ops), applied *symmetrically* to real and fake and preferably as clean/distorted pairs (TeleAI rank 3), is the most direct fix. Cost: augmentation code plus a fine-tuning phase, with no inference cost. Risk: some clean-data recall loss (top teams lost about 0.025 AUC clean-to-robust), and ViT-S capacity is far below DINOv3-7B, so expect a smaller gain than the winners.
- **DDA (NeurIPS 2025).** Reconstructed fakes have more high-frequency energy than JPEG reals. Matching the reconstruction's JPEG quality to its source real (probability 0.5) gives +10.5 points over the runner-up at JPEG Q60, +4.1 at 2× resize and +5.7 at blur σ 2 on GenImage-JPEG96. [V] — [DDA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14359v6)
- **B-Free (CVPR 2025).** With blur and JPEG augmentation plus aligned data, a social-network simulation (resize 0.7–1, JPEG Q70–100) leaves bAcc unchanged (86.3 to 86.4), and AUC goes from 99.3 to 98.5. Real social-network sets reach Facebook 95.6, Twitter 97.3 and Reddit 86.2 bAcc. The authors state augmentation helps most under resizing. [V] — [B-Free](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17671v2)
- **SPAI (CVPR 2025).** Removing JPEG augmentation costs 1.9 AUC, removing blur and noise costs 6.8 AUC, and adding WebP gives 89.3 against 91.0 (slightly worse). The authors concede that compression and noisy channels can erase spectral cues, and that recaptures and screenshots defeat it. [V] — [SPAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19417v2)
- **"Fake or JPEG?" (Grommelt et al., 2024).** GenImage reals are mostly JPEG and fakes are PNG, with systematic size differences. Detectors learn compression and size as the label. Removing the biases improves cross-generator generalisation. [A] — [arXiv 2403.17608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17608)
- **Panoptic Patch Learning (2025).** A CLIP or DINOv2 LoRA detector trained to use *all* patches stays near 90% accuracy under JPEG, blur and resize, including extreme settings. [V] — [arXiv 2504.01396](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01396v2)
- **Corvi et al. 2023 and Bammey 2023.** Periodic spectral and residual peaks of diffusion and GAN images are strongly attenuated by resizing and compression. [A] — [arXiv 2211.00680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00680); [Synthbuster DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/OJSP.2023.3337714)

### Inferences
- **Diagnosis for Tintype's NTIRE false positives.** Many NTIRE distortions add high-frequency energy: noise, impulse, ISO, sharpening-like tone curves, CLAHE, pixelation. If Tintype learned even partly that "excess high frequency = fake," which DDA shows is the default outcome of reconstruction or PNG fakes against JPEG reals, those distortions would push real images over threshold. A quick test: score NTIRE reals by distortion type and see whether the 155 flags concentrate on noise, sharpen, pixelation or neural-codec ops. If they do, symmetric augmentation with those ops is the fix.
- **Diagnosis for messenger misses.** WhatsApp and Instagram downscale to about 1600 px and apply JPEG around Q70–80. Tintype's native crops then see a resampled, requantised image whose high frequencies resemble a real JPEG. The model must rely on mid-frequency or semantic cues, which a 224-px ViT-S has less of. B-Free's flat social-network numbers came from (a) aligned data, so the model never learned to rely on high frequencies, plus (b) training-time resize and JPEG. Tintype already has (b), so (a) is the missing ingredient.
- Hand-crafted low-level streams should not be added to Tintype to fix robustness. The evidence (NTIRE top six, B-Free, DDA) says robustness comes from data alignment and distortion-symmetric augmentation.

### Gaps
- No paper found reports detector recall specifically on WhatsApp or Instagram round trips of 2025–26 commercial-generator images, other than B-Free's Facebook/Twitter/Reddit sets and the WasItAIGenerated benchmark already in docs/RESEARCH.md.
- The NTIRE report's per-distortion breakdown (which ops hurt most) was in the unread tail of the paper (beyond 100k characters) and was not extracted.

## Q4. How do low-level streams fuse with a semantic backbone, and what did two-stream designs buy?

### Takeaway
Two-stream designs reported in-distribution gains of a few points: AIDE +3.5/+4.6, and FatFormer with high GAN and diffusion accuracy from ProGAN-only training. Those gains largely *did not survive* independent 2025 evaluation on modern generators, where AIDE scored 53–64%. The fusion that did help in 2025–26 sits inside a single backbone: frequency-aware adapters, spectral-reconstruction statistics from the same ViT (SPAI), or learned patch aggregation. A separate CNN stream on SRM or DCT input adds cost and a shortcut to format bias.

### Cited Findings
- **Liu et al., "Forgery-aware Adaptive Transformer for Generalizable Synthetic Image Detection" (FatFormer), CVPR 2024.** A frozen CLIP ViT with a *forgery-aware adapter* that has an image branch and a frequency branch using DWT sub-bands, plus language-guided alignment. Trained on 4-class ProGAN, the abstract reports about 98% average accuracy on unseen GANs and about 95% on unseen diffusion models. [A] — [arXiv 2312.16649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16649). Independent: on ForenSynths (GANs) FatFormer scores 90.1, ahead of DDA's 81.4. [V] — [DDA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14359v6)
  - *Tintype applicability:* a DWT-frequency adapter inside Tintype's ViT-S blocks (parallel to attention, a few hundred thousand parameters) is the cheapest form of in-backbone frequency fusion. The evidence that it helps is strongest for **GANs**, Tintype's weak spot. Cost: medium (new adapter, Rust kernels for a Haar DWT, retrain). Speed cost: about 5–10% per view, my estimate. Risk: frequency branches re-introduce high-frequency shortcuts. Train it together with the NTIRE-style symmetric augmentation.
- **AIDE (ICLR 2025).** The CLIP semantic stream is fused with a ResNet over SRM-filtered highest- and lowest-frequency patches. In-distribution gains are +3.5 and +4.6. [V] — [arXiv 2406.19435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19435). Independent: 53.2 bAcc ([B-Free](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17671v2)) and 54.1 / 19.1 average / minimum ([DDA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14359v6)). [V]
- **SPAI (CVPR 2025).** Fuses spectral statistics *from the same ViT* (72-dim reconstruction similarity plus a 1024-dim context vector). Without the similarity statistics AUC drops from 91.0 to 71.0, and without the context vector to 84.9. The backbone choice matters: MFM ViT-B 91.0, CLIP ViT-B 87.6, DINOv2 ViT-B 87.5. [V] — [SPAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19417v2)
- **B-Free ablation.** End-to-end fine-tuning beats linear probing of frozen DINOv2 by 26.7 bAcc (95.2 against 68.5). [V] — [B-Free](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17671v2). This argues that a low-level signal is best learned *by* the backbone rather than fused from outside.
- **NTIRE 2026.** The winning designs are single-stream foundation models (DINOv3, EVA-CLIP, SigLIP2, MetaCLIP2) with ensembles. The only SRM-branch entry ranked below the top six. [V] — [NTIRE report](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11487)
- **"Raising the Bar…with CLIP" (Cozzolino et al., CVPRW 2024)**, already in docs/RESEARCH.md, is the semantic-only counterpoint.

### Inferences
- For Tintype, "fusion" is already happening in the best form: one ViT trained end to end on native crops (low-level) plus a fit view (semantic). Its photo second opinion (CF-384) is a model-level ensemble, not a stream fusion. Adding a hand-crafted stream contradicts the 2025–26 evidence.
- The one fusion idea with targeted support is a frequency adapter inside the ViT (FatFormer-style) for the GAN gap. A cheaper alternative with the same goal is GAN-data replay or distillation from CF-384 (see Q1).

### Gaps
- No paper found measures a two-stream design's *speed* overhead on a small ViT. The 5–10% figure above is my estimate, not a sourced number.

## Q5. Is native-resolution "most textured crop" selection supported or contradicted?

### Takeaway
It is **supported, with a caveat.** Direct evidence (TextureCrop) shows that selecting native-resolution crops by pixel standard deviation beats centre crop by about 3 bAcc, 5 AP and 6 AUC, and beats resizing by about 9 bAcc, 16 AP and 14 AUC, averaged over detectors on high-resolution Synthbuster and ForenSynths images. SPAI, B-Free and HiDA-Net all avoid resizing. The caveat: 2025 work shows that synthetic traces exist in *all* patches, and that detectors develop a "few-patch bias." Panoptic Patch Learning and SPAI find that learned aggregation over many patches beats a fixed handful averaged by the mean. AIDE and PatchCraft also exploit the *least*-textured patch. Tintype's design is right in direction, and the improvements are (a) to also include a flat patch, (b) to learn the aggregation, and (c) to regularise training against few-patch bias.

### Cited Findings
- **Konstantinidou, Koutlis & Papadopoulos, "TextureCrop: Enhancing Synthetic Image Detection through Texture-based Cropping," arXiv 2024 (CERTH).** Slides a 224×224 window with stride 200 and keeps crops whose grayscale standard deviation exceeds 0.1 (falling back to a centre crop). Mean aggregation is best, ahead of max and majority vote. FixedTextureCrop (top 15 crops by standard deviation) was the best fixed-count variant. Average gains across detectors: +3.13 bAcc, +5.17 AP and +5.71 AUC against centre crop; +9.33 / +15.78 / +13.96 against resize; +1.85 / +2.11 / +4.57 against ten-crop. The exception was DALL·E 3, where resizing was better. Tested on 6,462 images of at least 1024 px; no JPEG robustness test. [V] — [arXiv 2407.15500](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.15500v1)
  - *Tintype applicability:* this directly validates Tintype's "four most-textured crops" and mean-logit aggregation. Its best fixed count was 15 crops against Tintype's 4+1, which suggests that `--all-views` / `--sensitive` modes might gain recall from more textured crops, at proportional cost. The DALL·E 3 exception matches Tintype keeping a fit view.
- **Yang, Chen, Yan et al., "All Patches Matter, More Patches Better: Enhance AI-Generated Image Detection via Panoptic Patch Learning," arXiv 2504.01396 (2025; listed as ICLR 2026 in search results, venue not confirmed on the page).** Naively trained detectors concentrate on a few patches: masking a single patch cuts accuracy by 18.7 ± 4.1%. A single random patch tiled over an image is still detected about 90% of the time on SD v1.4.
  - Fixes: Random Patch Replacement (swap fake patches for real ones in training) and a patch-wise contrastive loss.
  - Results: GenImage mAcc 97.2 (CLIP) / 95.9 (DINOv2), against 95.6 for SAFE. DRCT-2M 99.5. Chameleon 69.3 / 72.1, about +5 over the prior SOTA. Near 90% under JPEG, blur and resize.
  - [V] — [arXiv 2504.01396](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01396v2)
  - *Tintype applicability:* Random Patch Replacement is a pure training-time augmentation. Paste real-photo patches into fake training images, label still fake, or use a patch-level mask target. It teaches the ViT to use evidence across the whole crop and makes the textured-crop views individually more reliable. It also builds toward **local-edit/inpainting** detection, a stated out-of-scope gap, because the model must find fake regions next to real ones. Cost: low. Risk: label noise if the replaced fraction is large. PPL keeps the fake label, so the ratio r needs tuning.
- **AIDE (ICLR 2025)** selects *both* the highest- and the lowest-frequency patches (DCT-scored) for its low-level stream. **PatchCraft (2023)** contrasts rich- and poor-texture regions. **Single Simple Patch (SSP, 2024)**, mentioned in the 2025 review, classifies from a single low-texture patch. [V for AIDE — [arXiv 2406.19435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19435); A for PatchCraft — [arXiv 2311.12397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12397); SSP via review — [arXiv 2502.15176](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.15176)]
  - *Tintype applicability:* add the *least-textured* non-blank crop as an optional view, ordered after the textured crops for early exit. Expected effect: possibly helps on generators whose flat regions carry the noise-floor anomaly (smooth, noise-free skies are a known tell of diffusion images). Test it on the blind sets. Risk: on JPEG copies, flat regions are block-artifact dominated and could raise false positives.
- **SPAI (CVPR 2025).** Learned spectral context attention over all 224 patches beats centre crop by 7.8 AUC (91.0 against 83.2) and beats ten-crop mean by 5.7. [V] — [SPAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19417v2)
- **B-Free (CVPR 2025)** tiles at native 504 px and averages logits. [V] — [B-Free](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17671v2). **HiDA-Net, "No Pixel Left Behind" (arXiv 2508.17346, 2025)** covers the whole image with native-resolution tiles and fuses their features, avoiding any downsampling (snippet-level only). [A] — [arXiv 2508.17346](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2508.17346). **"Structured Local Differential Modeling for AI-Generated Image Detection" (arXiv 2608.12811, 2026)** ranks patches by total-variation energy and keeps both the texture-complex and the texture-simple extremes (snippet-level only, not opened). [A] — [arXiv 2608.12811](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12811). **"Attention to Detail: Global-Local Attention for High-Resolution AI-Generated Image Detection" (arXiv 2601.00141, 2026)** pairs a global and a local branch for high-resolution input (title and snippet only). [A] — [arXiv 2601.00141](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00141v1)
- **NTIRE 2026 counterpoint.** The winners *resized* to 512 or 288 px at inference rather than tiling at native resolution. That makes sense because NTIRE inputs are already resampled and distorted, so native pixels carry no clean fingerprint. [V] — [NTIRE report](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11487)

### Inferences
- Texture-ranked native crops are well supported on *clean, high-resolution* images. On *already-resampled or distorted* images (messenger copies, NTIRE) the native-crop advantage shrinks. A larger-context fit view (Tintype's is 224 from 256) may matter more there. Possible change: when the input shows recompression evidence (JPEG quantisation tables matching WhatsApp or Instagram, long side at most 1600), order the fit view first and/or add a second fit view at a larger scale. That is a cheap routing tweak.
- Mean-logit aggregation over 6 views is a reasonable default (TextureCrop found mean the best fixed rule). SPAI's learned attention is better, but it conflicts with early exit. A compromise is a small learned weighting per view type (fit, centre, textured-k, flat), fitted on the development sets. That is cheap and keeps early exit.

### Gaps
- No paper directly compares "top-k by standard deviation" against "top-k by learned saliency" on *recompressed* inputs.
- HiDA-Net, Structured Local Differential Modeling and Attention-to-Detail were seen only as search snippets. Their numbers are not recorded here.

## Q6. Ranked shortlist: the most promising low-level ideas for Tintype

### Takeaway
The highest-value ideas from this literature are **training-data and augmentation changes with zero inference cost**: format and frequency alignment, NTIRE-style symmetric distortions, and inpainted or patch-replaced fakes. Next come a **cheap view and aggregation change** and, last, a **targeted GAN fix**. No hand-crafted spectral or residual stream is recommended for the default path.

### Cited Findings (ranked)

1. **Symmetric, NTIRE-style distortion chains, preferably as clean/distorted pairs, applied equally to real and fake.**
   - Evidence: the NTIRE 2026 top-6 used no frequency streams and won with heavy distortion augmentation (robust AUC 0.972 against clean 0.997); rank 3 trained on clean/distorted pairs ([NTIRE](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11487)). B-Free shows augmentation helps most under resizing ([B-Free](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17671v2)).
   - Targets: NTIRE recall (2.2% at 1% FPR) and NTIRE real false positives (155/600). Secondarily messenger copies.
   - Cost: low to medium (augmentation ops in the Rust training pipeline, including JPEG 2000 and ideally a neural codec, plus one fine-tuning phase). Inference cost: none.
   - Risk: some loss of clean recall, and limited ViT-S capacity.
2. **Format- and frequency-aligned hard fakes from Tintype's own real photos: VAE or self-conditioned reconstructions, JPEG quality matched as in DDA, plus pixel mixup.**
   - Evidence: DDA scores 90.7% average and 81.4% minimum over 11 benchmarks, +10.5 at JPEG Q60 ([DDA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14359v6)). B-Free's social-network bAcc stays flat under JPEG and resize ([B-Free](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17671v2)). AlignedForensics ([arXiv 2410.11835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11835)).
   - Targets: messenger recall, unseen-generator recall, and precision on content-confounded reals.
   - Cost: medium. It needs SD-VAE (and ideally FLUX-VAE) passes over the real training set offline; DDA's construction took 5.9 h. Inference cost: none.
   - Risk: without JPEG matching it teaches "high-frequency = fake," which would worsen NTIRE. Neural-codec-compressed reals may get flagged, so validate on NTIRE's JPEG AI and Cheng2020 reals.
3. **Inpainted fakes plus Random Patch Replacement (local-edit-aware training).**
   - Evidence: B-Free bAcc rises from 80.7 to 92.2 when inpainted fakes are added ([B-Free](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17671v2)). PPL shows few-patch bias (an 18.7% drop from masking one patch), and RPR raises Chameleon to about 70–72 with near-90% robustness ([PPL](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01396v2)).
   - Targets: the out-of-scope local inpainting gap, face swaps (14%), and per-view reliability, which makes early exit safer.
   - Cost: low for RPR (an augmentation) and medium for generating inpainted samples. Inference cost: none.
   - Risk: label design for partly-real images. Recall on full-generation sets could dip if r is too high.
4. **View-set and aggregation refinements.**
   - Changes: (a) keep texture-ranked native crops, which TextureCrop validates at +3.1 bAcc and +5.7 AUC against centre crop. (b) Add an optional least-textured crop, as in AIDE and PatchCraft. (c) Learn per-view-type weights instead of a plain mean, a light version of SPAI's patch attention, which beat ten-crop mean by 5.7 AUC. (d) For images with messenger-like JPEG tables or size, put the fit view first and consider a larger-scale fit view.
   - Evidence: [TextureCrop](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.15500v1), [SPAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19417v2), [AIDE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19435), [NTIRE](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11487) (winners resized distorted inputs).
   - Targets: recall at equal speed; early-exit efficiency on recompressed images.
   - Cost: low. Risk: a flat crop on JPEG copies may raise false positives, so gate it.
5. **A targeted GAN fix: replay or distillation before any frequency adapter.**
   - Evidence: DDA's own GAN weakness (ForenSynths 81.4, against FatFormer 90.1 with its DWT-frequency adapter) shows that aligned and diffusion-centric training loses GAN coverage, while frequency-aware designs keep it ([DDA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14359v6); [FatFormer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16649)).
   - For Tintype, the cheapest step is GAN-set replay or distillation from the CF-384 checkpoint during fine-tuning. Only if that fails, add a small DWT adapter, or a 1.9M-parameter FreqNet-style GAN specialist as a cheap second opinion in place of CF-384 ([FreqNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07240)).
   - Targets: GAN recall (84–89%). Possibly speed, if a tiny specialist replaces the CF-384 second opinion.
   - Risk: frequency branches are fragile under JPEG.

**Not recommended for Tintype:**
- Test-time diffusion inversion or reconstruction (DIRE, LaRE², FIRE, Manifold-bias): hundreds of times too slow.
- Hand-crafted NPR, LGrad or SRM streams: these collapse on modern generators (42–65% in independent 2025 tables).
- SPAI as a second stage: about ViT-B ×3 cost, with self-declared compression fragility.
- RIGID or ZED: candidates only as experimental extra features for the second-opinion fusion, not as detectors.

### Inferences
- Items 1–3 can be combined in one fine-tuning phase and leave Tintype's inference speed unchanged. Item 4 is speed-neutral to slightly slower. Item 5 could make the second opinion faster.
- The expected direction of effect is grounded in the cited papers. The magnitude for a ViT-S at 224 px is not: every headline number above comes from ViT-B/L, DINOv2 or DINOv3-class backbones at 336–512 px.

### Gaps
- No source measures these interventions on a ViT-S/224 starting from the Community Forensics checkpoint. Gains must be measured on Tintype's own development and blind protocol.
- Whether Tintype's current training already contains PNG-fake / JPEG-real format bias was not examined here; the training mix is documented in docs/TRAINING.md and docs/DATA.md. That check should come before item 2.
