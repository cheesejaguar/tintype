# Train through distortion first: ten verified Tintype upgrades

## Executive summary: ten changes ranked by impact, confidence and cost

| Rank | Change | Metric targeted | Cost (training / inference / engineering) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Class-symmetric chained degradation curriculum. Cheap pixel ops in `augment()` first. Offline codec stores and a paired consistency loss come second, as ablation arms gated on a per-operation audit | NTIRE distorted AUROC (0.636), recall at 1% FPR (2.2%), real FPR (25.8%); messenger recall (27–36%); JPEG-stress recall | Retrain plus re-finalize / none / medium (high once the codec stores and paired loader are added) | Medium-high for the augmentation; low for the paired loss |

Tintype's largest measured weakness is **processing history**: noise, codecs, resizing and re-encoding. Generator novelty matters less. Retraining the ViT-S/16 through a **class-symmetric chained degradation curriculum** is still the highest-impact change. On the NTIRE 2026 distorted public test, Tintype's AUROC falls from **0.870 clean to 0.636**, a drop of 0.23. Every top-eight entrant lost only 0.03–0.12 ([NTIRE 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11487v1)). All of them trained through heavy chained distortions. Verification narrowed the recommendation in three ways. The paired consistency loss becomes an ablation arm: TeleAI's clean/distorted KL alone added only **+0.15 AUC** ([LPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12307v1)), and the top two teams did not use one. The entrants' 0.834–0.972 range is an in-domain upper bound, not a target. And lossy WebP, AVIF and JPEG 2000 cannot be produced with the repo's pure-Rust `image` crate. The cheapest verified win is rank 2. On the archived batch-7 scores, raising the second-opinion band's lower edge from −7 to −3 cuts the photos sent to Community Forensics **from 48.0% to 10.5%**. Pooled recall at the same 329 false positives does not fall (0.9405 vs 0.935). That is a four-number spec edit. Verification also refuted or demoted parts of four draft recommendations: learned and symmetric second-opinion fusion, video early-exit hardening, the face detector and face-gated view, and INT8 as a cheap change. The literature never tests single-image, quality-stratified, real-only thresholds, so calibration (rank 3) drops to low-medium confidence. Every effect size here is an extrapolation from ViT-B/L, CLIP or DINO backbones at 224–512 px. Every new set of weights invalidates Tintype's calibration, early-exit margins and second-opinion band, and needs a **fresh pre-registered blind batch**: batch 7, NTIRE, Arena and Frontier are all development data now. The evidence base is **267 distinct academic papers, reports and benchmarks**: 231 from the first eight notes, 27 from gap fill, and 9 added during verification.

## Tintype fails on processing history more than on new generators

The repository's own measurements locate the losses. On clean, current-generator photos Tintype is strong: precision 0.988 and recall 0.897 on blind batch 7 (`docs/BENCHMARK.md:185`), and 0.997 / 0.941 on the blind video batch (`docs/BENCHMARK.md:881`). It degrades when an image has been processed, on a handful of generator families, and on stock video.

Four codebase facts shape every recommendation. First, `augment()` (`src/data.rs:57-104`) has only JPEG before and after resizing, a random resampling kernel, mild scale jitter, a flip, a messenger round trip (long side 960–1700 px, quality 55–90, p = 0.25 in photo phases 2, 3 and 6) and H.264 re-encoding for video. It takes no label, so it is class-symmetric. It interleaves degradation with the random crop, so two calls do not give spatially aligned views. Second, calibration is one real-quantile threshold averaged over real sources with at least 100 items (Balanced(100)), fitted on all six views (`src/eval.rs:276-337, 584-589`). Third, nothing anchors fine-tuning to the CF-224 initialisation (`src/trainer.rs:131-136`), and there is no weight EMA. Fourth, training, evaluation and the GPU engine are macOS-only. The trainer is MPSGraph with no PyTorch, so every training-side change is Rust work or an external pipeline (`src/main.rs:653-656`).

Several draft-stage claims fail against the code and are not used below. Training uses one random view per sample, not the six texture-ranked views (`src/data.rs:80-94`). The 15.8k-vs-3.0k Reddit imbalance does not become a 5:1 sampling ratio under the √count sampler. NTIRE's failure is not "mainly threshold transfer". The archived score files hold probabilities only, although `evaluate` and `finalize` write all-view logits locally (`src/eval.rs:414-420, 583-585`). Pexels and OpenVid stock reals are already video negatives; only Pixabay is missing.

## Distortion and calibration: recommendations 1–3

### 1. Train through chained, class-symmetric degradations, then test codecs and pairing as ablations

**Concrete change.** Split the work into two stages. **Stage (a)** extends `augment()` in `src/data.rs` with a chained sampler that draws 1–5 operations at graded severity, in a curriculum of clean, mild, moderate and heavy levels. All of its operations are cheap enough for the 11 loader threads: Gaussian, Poisson, speckle, impulse and ISO-like noise; Gaussian, motion and lens blur; unsharp sharpening; pixelation and downscale-upscale chains; and JPEG. Widen the messenger round trip to caps of **720–2048 px** on width, height or long side, following TrueFake's measured platform caps. Chain **1–6 share cycles**, weighted toward fewer, so that RRDataset's 2–6 range is covered. Colour and tone operations go into a **separate ablation arm**, not the default curriculum. **Stage (b)**, gated on the audit below, adds lossy WebP, AVIF and JPEG 2000 round trips, neural codecs (JPEG AI, CompressAI Cheng2020 and hyperprior at 0.06–0.5 bpp) and invisible-watermark embedders. These must be **pre-encoded offline as paired stores of both classes**, or WebP and AVIF can be bound through macOS ImageIO via the `objc2` crates already used for AVFoundation. The `image` crate here has no lossy WebP encoder (image-webp 0.2.4 is lossless only) and no AVIF or JPEG 2000 support (`Cargo.toml:18`; `Cargo.lock:2181-2183`). The offline stores need a **source/pair id** in the manifest and `Item` (`src/ingest.rs:16-22`), and the train/validation split must hash on that id rather than the path. Otherwise codec copies of calibration images leak into training (`src/data.rs:192-199`). Stage (b) also tests a paired consistency loss as an ablation, not a default. That needs `augment()` refactored into a shared-geometry stage and a degradation stage, a loader that emits pairs, a 2B batch split with `slice` in `src/trainer.rs`, and CLI weights for the term. Use a **stop-gradient clean teacher** (one-directional KL, DCPT's actual form) so that clean logits are not pulled toward less confident degraded ones.

**Targeted weak spot and metrics.** NTIRE distorted AUROC (0.636), recall at 1% FPR (2.2%) and real FPR at the shipped cutoff (25.83%). Messenger recall (27–36%). Frontier JPEG-960 recall (28.0%) and Arena-JPEG recall (73.1%).

**Evidence.** Every top-eight NTIRE 2026 robust-track team trained through heavy chained distortions. The top two, MICV and Ant International, reached robust AUROC **0.9723 and 0.9721** (averaged over splits) against clean 0.9974 and 0.9972. Their method was curriculum or offline multi-level augmentation alone, with no paired loss. Of the top six, none used a frequency or noise-residual stream. Only Reagvis (7th) had an SRM/Bayar branch ([NTIRE 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11487v1)). Entrant averages ran 0.834–0.972. The per-split public-test distorted values the repo reads from the same table run 0.8302–0.9738 (`docs/EVIDENCE.md:200`). Two caveats keep this from being a target. Entrants trained in-domain on NTIRE's training split, with a distortion function sharing families with the test. And Tintype's clean NTIRE AUROC (0.870) is below every top-eight entrant's clean score (lowest 0.9227), so part of the gap is domain or generator rather than distortion. The distortion premise survives, because Tintype's clean-to-robust drop of 0.23 is two to eight times any entrant's. The most transferable single data point is vincentlc: one SigLIP2-giant with a linear head, the official distortion function applied to every training image, robust AUROC **0.873**, an augmentation its authors called "the key driver". Shallow Real is the warning case at **0.995 clean but 0.834 robust** ([NTIRE 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11487v1)). SPAI loses **6.8 AUC** without blur and noise augmentation (91.0 → 84.2) ([SPAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19417v2)). Konstantinidou et al. measured augmentation lifting in-the-wild social-media AUC from **82.20 to 92.26 for DMID** and from 91.46 to 98.10 for SPAI. The same paper shows mismatched augmentation hurting, with RINE on Chameleon at 92.16 → 87.33 and NPR on Synthbuster at 72.35 → 64.08. Changing the training data moved RINE far more (83.5 → 97.7) than augmentation did (about 4.5 points) ([Konstantinidou et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10236v1)). For platform pipelines, TrueFake measured Facebook capping width at 720 px with quality 61–92, X at 1200 px with quality 87 (and no recompression at or below 768×768), and Telegram capping height at 800 px with quality 85. Each platform was measured with one upload and download ([TrueFake](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20658)). RRDataset showed 2–6 share cycles collapsing SAFE's fake accuracy from **98.29 to 0.88** while real accuracy rose to 98.85. That is a collapse toward "real", matching Tintype's messenger recall failure. RRDataset credits its robust detectors to diffusion-based features, not to augmentation ([RRDataset](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09172)).

The codec evidence is milder than the draft said. At 0.12 bpp JPEG AI, Cannas et al. measured pristine-image FPR of **0.96–0.99 for four low-level CNN/NPR detectors**, 0.42–0.72 for the CLIP-based ones closer to Tintype's ViT, and 0.16 for Wang-B. Retraining one face-trained EfficientNet with 20% JPEG AI cut FPR from 0.96 to 0.65 but raised FNR from 0.05 to 0.14 ([Cannas et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03261v2)). The paired-loss evidence is weak on ranking metrics. In TeleAI's LPT entry, KL alone added **+0.15** public-test AUC (87.91 → 88.06). The full +1.35 needed an extra MSE term through a learned corrector FFN, measured over clean and distorted images together, in a single run ([LPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12307v1)). DCPT's widely quoted 64.0 → 73.1 is accuracy at a fixed threshold. On ranking, degraded AUC rose only **78.5 → 79.4** while clean AUC fell 89.1 → 87.9. In the same paper, B-Free, trained with plain augmentation and no consistency loss, scored higher on degraded accuracy, 82.5 against 73.1 ([DCPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10102v1)).

**Expected effect on Tintype (extrapolated).** Stage (a) is the most plausible route to shrinking the clean-to-distorted AUROC drop without leaving 224 px. It should also lift messenger and JPEG-stress recall. Reaching the entrants' 0.83–0.97 is unlikely. They trained in-domain on larger backbones, and part of Tintype's NTIRE gap is domain. The effect on neural-codec false positives is **unknown**. CLIP-like detectors are less codec-fragile than low-level CNNs, and retraining on codecs helped only partly in the one measured case. The audit below decides whether stage (b) is worth building. Most of the paired loss's benefit, if any, will appear as calibration stability at the cutoff rather than as better ranking.

**Cost.** Training only; no inference change. Stage (a) is days of Rust plus a retrain from phase-6 weights, then re-finalize. Stage (b) is a multi-file change: `src/data.rs`, `src/image_io.rs`, `src/trainer.rs`, `src/train_cmd.rs`, `src/main.rs` and `src/ingest.rs`, plus an offline encoding job and possibly an ImageIO binding. The paired loss doubles forward and backward cost per step. Watch loader throughput through `train_cmd`'s "data wait %". New weights also require re-finalize, re-checking the hard-coded `EarlyExit::standard()` margins with all-view scores, re-searching or disabling the photo second-opinion band, and a **fresh pre-registered blind batch** (`src/eval.rs:485-500`; `docs/PREREGISTRATION.md:185-197, 244-250`).

**Risks.** Tuning on the test set: NTIRE, Frontier and Arena are development-visible, so modelling the curriculum on NTIRE's operations and reporting NTIRE gains is not a blind result. Augmentation that mismatches deployment can lower AUC, as with RINE and NPR above. Clean-recall loss, the Shallow Real pattern; clean recall at 0.5% FPR (strict profile) should be a non-regression gate. SPAI found chromatic augmentation cut AUC from 91.0 to 80.5 and could not explain why, which is why colour is a separate arm. Heavy degradation erases evidence and acts as label noise for both classes.

**Cheapest first experiment.** Step 0 is nearly free. Extend `scripts/diagnose-ntire.py` so that its per-operation FPR and recall breakdown covers every NTIRE operation, not only the three artifact operations, using the archived scores and the organisers' label CSVs. Step 1: extend `scripts/stress-photo.py`, today a JPEG-only paired manifest generator, to write paired per-operation manifests for about 2,000 reals and 2,000 fakes. Include learned super-resolution, a whole-image VAE round trip and INP-X-style partial VAE passes as well as the candidate operations ([Coccomini et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.02670); [INP-X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00192)). Then run `tintype score --all-views` and report **both** the real-FPR shift and AUROC per operation. Operations that raise both classes, like the NTIRE artifact group, are shortcut candidates. Step 2 is a roughly 4k-step stage (a) phase from phase-6 weights, followed by `finalize`, with NTIRE hard-validation distorted recall at 1% FPR (baseline 5.4%) and AUROC (0.734) reported only as development re-scores. Before drawing a confirmation batch, check that batch-7 clean recall and benchmarks without NTIRE-style distortions hold.

### 2. Narrow the photo second-opinion band and judge it at matched FPR

**Concrete change.** Raise the band's lower edge `lo` from −7 to about −3 in calibrated logits. Keep `hi` at or near the decision threshold (0.5), and re-select `weight` and `threshold` by grid search on the 48 development photo sets under Balanced(100) at 1.5% FPR. Ship the new values through the existing `set-photo-second-opinion`, which stores exactly `lo, hi, weight, threshold` (`src/main.rs:197-204, 655-665`; `src/detector.rs:220-236`). That needs no engine change. **Drop** the draft's learned logistic fusion with view-spread and views-used features, and drop the symmetric "veto" band. The archived batch-7 scores refute the veto. The extra features cannot be built from the archive, are near-redundant with |z1| under early exit, and would need struct, scan-path and calibrate changes.

**Targeted weak spot and metrics.** Second-opinion throughput (281–288 photos/s on GPU against 530–563 without it). Pooled recall and real FPR at matched false-positive counts. Sets at or above Community Forensics on both precision and recall: 26/30 today, the band's design objective.

**Evidence.** The shipped rule sends **48.0% of batch-7 photos** to stage 2, including 89% of reals. Nearly all of that routing comes from `lo = −7`. Applying the stored rule to `logit(tintype)` and `logit(commfor)` from `docs/results/confirm7-scores.tsv.gz` reproduces the shipped `tintype-second` column with zero mismatches, so band experiments run anywhere, including Linux. At the shipped fusion's 329 false positives, re-thresholded and in-sample:

| Configuration | Recall | Precision | Photos routed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tintype default alone, re-thresholded (p > 0.2206) | 0.932 | 0.972 | 0% |
| `--sensitive` column alone, re-thresholded | 0.948 | 0.973 | 0% |
| Shipped fusion, band (−7, 0.5) | 0.935 | 0.972 | 48.0% |
| Band (−3, 0.5) | **0.9405** | 0.972 | **10.5%** |
| Bands with `hi = 3` (veto), e.g. (−3, 3), (−7, 3) | 0.888–0.927 | — | more |

The pooled AUROC is **0.987 with or without the second opinion** (`docs/BENCHMARK.md:183-186`). On the pooled measure, the fusion is mostly a move along the same ROC curve. Its real value is per set: 26 of 30 batch-7 sets reach or beat CF on both precision and recall, against 12/30 for the default profile and at most 14/30 for any single-network threshold. In the real run, the upper part of the band cleared 59 AI photos to remove 25 real ones (`docs/BENCHMARK.md:228-235`). Community Forensics vetoes more true positives than false ones. The audio-fusion literature favours keeping the simple calibrated-sum rule rather than a learned combiner. In Serrano et al.'s Table IV, summing calibrated LLRs beat logistic-regression fusion on average out-of-domain EER in all four configurations (for example 8.3 vs 7.7) ([fusion of SSL countermeasures](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12003)). Logistic-regression fusion helped in-domain in a 36-detector ASVspoof5 study ([multi-objective fusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01330)). Wisdom of Committees supports symmetric max-confidence gating with thresholds chosen on held-out data. Its 2.3× speed-up does not transfer, because Tintype's second stage is a *weaker* model added on top ([Wisdom of Committees](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.01988)).

**Expected effect on Tintype (extrapolated).** Stage-2 work falls about 4.6×. A linear cost model fitted to the two measured throughputs puts second-opinion mode at roughly **450 photos/s, up from 281–288**, unmeasured. Pooled recall at matched FPR should be unchanged or slightly better. The batch-7 numbers are in-sample, so a development-set fit may land elsewhere. The default profile is untouched, because the second opinion is opt-in. A plausible outcome is that the pooled gain over a re-thresholded single network is near zero. Then the mode's justification is per-set parity with CF, and the docs should say so.

**Cost.** Hours. No training, no new code for the band; inference is cheaper.

**Risks.** Batch 7 is spent: the sweep above is in-sample. Fit on the 48 development sets, use batch 7 only as a labelled-seen sanity check, and pre-register before fetching a batch 8 (`docs/PREREGISTRATION.md:166-232`). `report --at-rate-of` per-set rows use an oracle threshold per set. Use the `--pooled` row or `scripts/report-archive.py --at-rate-of` (`src/report.rs:84-121`). Recalibrating Tintype later (recommendations 1, 4, 7) invalidates the band again.

**Cheapest first experiment.** From the archive, sweep `lo ∈ {−7, −5, −4, −3, −2}` with `hi` at 0.5. Report recall at 329 false positives, sets at or above CF on both measures, and routed share. Then repeat the sweep on the development sets' score files (`runs/scores` on the training Mac), choose under Balanced(100), and store the result with `set-photo-second-opinion`. Report `--sensitive` (0.963 / 0.960 at 4.3% observed FPR on batch 7, against a 5% target) as the cheap competitor.

### 3. Diagnose per-stratum FPR, add a pre-registered UNSURE, and gate the degradation head

**Concrete change.** Start with an offline diagnostic, not a threshold change. Extend `scripts/diagnose-ntire.py`, which already joins scores with resolution, quantisation tables and distortion labels, to fit thresholds per **coarse stratum**: two to four cells, for example short side below or at least 512 px, crossed with estimated JPEG quality below or at least about 80. Report how many calibration reals fall in each cell, and report maximum-over-sources FPR with Clopper–Pearson upper bounds next to the Balanced(100) average. Add a Clopper–Pearson routine to `src/metrics.rs`, which has bootstrap intervals only. Only then, if the diagnostic justifies it, implement strata as **per-stratum (scale, bias) pairs in `ModelSpec`**. Calibrated logit 0 then stays the threshold everywhere, and the early-exit margins and second-opinion band keep their meaning (`src/detector.rs:121-155, 214-235`). Add an `UNSURE` verdict in `src/scan.rs` and `src/server.rs` for sparse strata, with a **pre-registered abstention rule** and coverage reporting. Update `docs/API.md`, whose verdict set is a public contract, and `docs/PREREGISTRATION.md`. JPEG quantisation tables need a small DQT-marker parser, because `image` 0.25 does not expose them. The learned degradation head (regressing applied JPEG quality, scale, noise and blur sigma, and a learned-codec flag) is a **separate retraining project**. It needs the noise and blur augmentations of recommendation 1, offline codec pairs, a second placeholder and multi-task loss in the trainer, and a second output in `src/cpu_vit.rs` and `src/mps_vit.rs`. Gate it like recommendation 1(b), and hold NTIRE out of codec-flag training and selection.

**Targeted weak spot and metrics.** Real FPR on degraded inputs (NTIRE public distorted 25.83%; hard validation 16.48%). Truthfulness of the per-source FPR claim. Coverage and precision with abstention.

**Evidence.** The repo shows both the value and the limits. Moving the cutoff fitted on hard-validation distorted reals at 1% FPR to the public test gave **FPR 2.17%, double its target, and recall 4.77%** (`docs/EVIDENCE.md:212-218`). A threshold fitted on a neighbouring processing distribution misses its nominal FPR. Metadata strata are partly informative. All 5,000 NTIRE files share one quantisation table and 4:2:0 subsampling, so quality strata are blind there. Resolution separates somewhat: 28.18% FPR below 512 px against 23.62% at 512 px and above. In the neural-codec group, authentic FPR (60.44%) roughly equals recall (63.18%), and **no threshold fixes a detector with no ranking**. The only honest output there is UNSURE, which needs a learned-codec flag (`transfer-diagnostics.txt:16-40`). In the literature, QuAD models logit mean and variance per class as a function of a no-reference quality score. Against naive multi-copy aggregation it raised six-detector balanced accuracy on AncesTree from 73.2% to 81.6% and cut NLL from 0.75 to 0.43. The single-image baseline was 70.7% with NLL 0.97 ([QuAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15027v1)). Every QuAD result aggregates several web copies. Single-image per-stratum thresholds are untested, so QuAD supports them only by analogy. LAION-Mobile's **17–91%** false-positive rates on real phone photos come from equal-error-rate thresholds fitted on NTIRE, where no detector beat AUC 0.624. The paper shows ranking collapse and score-scale drift. It does not test real-only per-stratum thresholds ([LAION-Mobile](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11134)). For abstention, ReSIDe showed max-softmax confidence is worse than random under blur: AURC 0.7276 against an error rate of 0.4950, with a ResNet50 at chance. Intermediate-layer probes tuned on 1,000 labelled same-family images cut it to 0.2406 (MSP) or 0.2214 (max-logit) ([ReSIDe](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08574)). The closest precedent for a learned "can I handle this input" signal is DACOM, which uses distortion-aware confidence for selective abstention and routing ([DACOM](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10010162)). HiDA-Net's JPEG-quality regression head, trained on FBCNN pseudo-labels, added only +0.8 accuracy in its GenImage ablation (93.92 → 94.73), and its use as a stratum is untested ([HiDA-Net](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17346v1)). The statistics are standard. Split conformal gives marginal coverage over the calibration mixture ([Angelopoulos & Bates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511)), so a Balanced(100) average covers an equal-weighted mixture of calibration sources, not the deployment mix. Per-stratum (Mondrian) validity holds only within a stratum under exchangeability, with the stratum rule fixed independently of the data. Guarantees degrade under shift ([Barber et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13415); [Tibshirani et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06019)), and strong conditional coverage is impossible distribution-free ([Barber et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04684)). A degradation head that mis-assigns unseen processing silently breaks the guarantee.

**Expected effect on Tintype (extrapolated).** An honest precision claim: maximum per-source FPR with upper bounds, and an UNSURE path for inputs the detector cannot rank. FPR on degraded reals falls within covered strata, but on NTIRE's own numbers only by giving up recall. Recall rises only if paired with recommendation 1. UNSURE coverage on degraded inputs could be large. The calibration reals (training-store validation splits and development benchmarks) contain few NTIRE-like degraded reals, so the target strata are exactly the ones short of data.

**Cost.** The diagnostic and Clopper–Pearson reporting are low cost. Strata in `ModelSpec`, UNSURE and the protocol and API changes are moderate. The degradation head is high cost and macOS-only. Runtime cost of DQT parsing and one extra linear layer is negligible.

**Risks.** Sample size: at 100 reals per stratum, the 95% Clopper–Pearson upper bound for 1 false positive is about 5.4%, so a 1–1.5% target needs several hundred reals per stratum. An attacker can degrade a fake into a lenient stratum, so uncovered strata must map to UNSURE, never to "real". Contaminated "real" pools (AI images inside social reals) mis-set thresholds most in the social strata ([Hataya et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08095)). Max-over-sources is stricter than Balanced(100) and would lower recall on every profile if adopted as the rule rather than reported.

**Cheapest first experiment.** Offline, on archived scores: compute `logit(p)`, a monotone transform, so cutoffs on p are equivalent. Fit per-stratum cutoffs on hard-validation distorted reals by resolution bucket and, where defined, operation group. Apply them to the public test, and tabulate FPR, recall and per-stratum Clopper–Pearson bounds against the single transferred cutoff (2.17% / 4.77%). Do the same for the WhatsApp/Instagram copies by long side. The Rust `calibrate` path cannot read the merged multi-system archives, because columns 5 onward are parsed as view logits (`src/report.rs:17-31`). That makes this a script, not a subcommand run.

## Data, views and video precision: recommendations 4–6

### 4. Make real and fake differ only in generator traces, across every decoder family

**Concrete change.** Five steps, run in this order. **(i) Audit.** Freeze the current manifests with the existing `freeze-set`. Write a small script under `scripts/`, reusing the file-signature sniffer `image_format` in `scripts/evaluate-arena.py:113` and adding DQT-based quality estimation, chroma subsampling and EXIF presence. Report per class and per store: container, quality, table family, subsampling, resolution and EXIF. Start with `tiny-genimage`, whose ImageNet reals are JPEG and whose fakes are mostly PNG ([GenImage](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.08571v2); [Fake or JPEG?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17608)). In the same pass, score every real store with Tintype and CF-384 and hand-inspect the top 0.5% of reals, to estimate AI contamination per source ([Hataya et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08095)). **(ii) Write a labelling policy before generating anything.** Decide what VAE-laundered reals, neural-codec reals, AI-upscaled reals and AI-edited reals are. Make NTIRE authentic FPR on the neural-codec subgroup a pre-stated guardrail whose rise stops the experiment. **(iii) Final-decoder reconstructions.** Build offline reconstructions of Tintype's reals through 4-channel (SD 1.x/SDXL) and 16-channel (SD3/FLUX) VAEs, open VQ/BSQ tokenizers (LlamaGen, Janus-Pro, Infinity) and open learned upsamplers. Re-encode with probability 0.5 at the source real's estimated JPEG quality, as DDA does. Batch each reconstruction **with its source real under shared crop and augmentation** (Aligned's "Sync" variant). **(iv) Open AR generators.** Add the seven ARForensics models (LlamaGen, VAR, Infinity, Janus-Pro, RAR, Switti, Open-MAGVIT2), first as held-out evaluation, then as training data with one tokenizer family held out. **(v) Real domains.** Add smartphone photos with EXIF, camera originals, screenshots and social-platform reals, de-duplicated perceptually against the evaluation suites. Generation runs in an external PyTorch pipeline. It can run on the existing Mac through MPS: hours to about a day for 50–100k VAE reconstructions. To keep provenance, host the outputs with pinned revisions behind a new plan in `src/plans.rs` and an entry in `src/fetch.rs`. Defer B-Free-style regional inpaints. They change README's declared scope (`README.md:510-512`), and a random native crop of a mostly-restored image is label noise without a mask column in the manifest.

**Targeted weak spot and metrics.** Arena-JPEG (73.1%) and Frontier JPEG-960 (28.0%) recall. Clean recall on GPT-image-2 (0.58), Seedream 3.0 (0.52) and Nano Banana (0.59). Messenger recall. FPR on unusual reals.

**Evidence.** Aligned reconstructions lifted post-processed recall on Playground from **24.27% (Corvi-style baseline) to 61.25% unpaired and 76.36% with paired "Sync" batching**, and on LCM from 58.31% to 90.12% and 91.08%. FLUX.1-dev was caught 9.6% (unpaired), 25.9% (Sync) and 3.2% (Corvi) by detectors trained on a 4-channel LDM autoencoder. The authors attribute this to the VAE's channel count but did not test 16-channel training. They also report that reconstructions lack the reals' WebP artifacts, so the detector is not fully robust to WebP; no magnitude is given ([Aligned Datasets](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11835v3)). DDA's JPEG-matched recipe reached **90.7% average and 81.4% minimum accuracy over 11 benchmarks**, and led by 10.5 points at quality 60 on GenImage-JPEG96. Building it took **5.9 hours**, as DDA measured. Its ForenSynths score (81.4) trails FatFormer (90.1) and C2P-CLIP (92.1), which DDA attributes partly to those baselines' ProGAN training overlap. DDA used a LoRA-tuned DINOv2 at 336 px ([DDA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14359v6)). B-Free's large gains require **diffusion-step self-conditioned regeneration**. Plain autoencoder reconstructions scored 80.7 balanced accuracy, and 83.9 with inpaints added. Self-conditioned fakes went 81.4 → 92.2 with inpaints → 95.2 → 96.4 with more augmentation, at about 3 s per image against 0.18 s for an autoencoder pass ([B-Free](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17671v2); [DDA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14359v6)). Removing JPEG and size bias alone gained **more than 11 points** cross-generator ([Fake or JPEG?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17608)). Gap-fill work widens the decoder idea. Training on reals passed through three categories of generator final components, 100 samples each, reportedly reached **98.83% over 22 unseen-generator sets** (abstract only) ([Final Component](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20461)). In PRADA's comparison on open AR text-to-image models, the broadly trained B-Free scores **99.8 AUROC**, against D³QE's 76.5 ([PRADA](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20068v2)). That points to a data gap rather than an architecture gap. ARForensics also shows tokenizer families differ: D³QE manages only 62.88% on bitwise-tokenizer Infinity ([D³QE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05891)). GPT-ImgEval attributes GPT-4o's detectability to an internal upsampling stage ([GPT-ImgEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02782v3)). Messenger downscaling would destroy that trace first. One caution: FLUX.1 Pro, Seedream 3.0 and Recraft v3 are already in Tintype's `frontier-heldout` training store since phase 3 (`docs/DATA.md:94`), so their low recall is not purely missing data. On real domains, ScaleDF's power law (1−AUC = 0.729·N_real^−0.476) is **face-deepfake evidence** and only directional for Tintype ([ScaleDF](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16320)). Community Forensics found real-image composition made "marginal" differences ([Community Forensics](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04125)). New real domains are therefore justified by Tintype's own false-positive and messenger failures, not by scaling laws.

**Expected effect on Tintype (extrapolated).** A smaller recall drop when both classes are re-encoded, better recall on latent-diffusion and AR generators that reuse open decoders, and fewer format shortcuts. With plain autoencoder reconstructions, expect B-Free's 80–84 regime, not its 92–96. Paired batching is worth as much as the reconstructions themselves in Aligned's results. Neural-codec false positives may rise, which is why the guardrail is a stop condition.

**Cost.** Offline generation plus a retrain (8k steps at phase-6 scale) and re-finalize. The √count sampler cannot reach a 20–30% share for a new store: one 50k-image key gets about 1.7% of AI draws among about 1,200 keys. Add a **per-key or per-group mixture weight** to `Sampler::new` and `sample` in `src/data.rs`, with flags in `src/main.rs` and `src/train_cmd.rs`. New real stores join the calibration pool through `finalize`. Easy new sources lower the Balanced(100) average and loosen the threshold on hard ones, so report per-source FPR before and after.

**Risks.** The laundering conflict: Mandelli et al. showed a real photo passed through Stable Diffusion's autoencoder reads as synthetic ([SD laundering](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.10736)). INP-X showed **97% of an inpainted image's background spectral shift comes from the VAE round trip**. When the original background is restored, DDA, which is trained on VAE reconstructions, falls from 97.6% to 58.4% ([INP-X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00192)). Reconstruction training teaches a global decoder-trace shortcut, and that can worsen Tintype's largest false-positive cluster. Mixup is unresolved. B-Free saw balanced accuracy fall from 81.4 to 78.6 but AUC rise from 94.7 to 95.3, which is a calibration effect, while DDA's paired pixel mixup is a different operation. Every new store invalidates batch 7 as a blind test.

**Cheapest first experiment.** Run the step (i) audit on `tiny-genimage` and the largest stores. Reconstruct 5,000 held-out reals through an SD VAE, a FLUX VAE and a LlamaGen VQ tokenizer, and compress the same reals with JPEG AI and Cheng2020. Score all of them with the shipped weights. A high flag rate on reconstructions means little headroom. The gap between reconstruction and codec scores measures the labelling conflict. Score ARForensics and the GPT Image2 / Nano Banana2 set ([benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01258)) as held-out tests.

### 5. Re-derive view pooling and order offline, and stop scoring identical views

**Concrete change.** Build a small **replay tool**, either a Rust subcommand or a script. `apply_profile` (`src/eval.rs:365-392`) is private, fits nothing, and hard-codes the mean, and no code fits exit margins. The tool should (a) replay any pooling rule and view order over stored per-view logits, (b) search exit margins under the existing rule that pooled precision and recall stay within 0.001, and (c) refit scale and bias per rule. Feed it the per-view logits that `evaluate` and `finalize` already write locally (`scores/<set>.<tag>-views.tsv`, `scores/calib_images.tsv`). Re-score only NTIRE hard validation with `--all-views`. Compare the plain mean against top-2, trimmed mean, log-mean-exp, native-only mean and per-view-type weights. Test fit-first against fit-last split by recompression evidence. Test a least-textured non-flat window **as a swap for rich-3**, not a seventh view, and Global Histogram Entropy as an alternative texture measure. Add a text-density condition. Ship separately the no-risk speed fix: when `min(w, h) < 224`, every native view is the same bilinear upscale (`src/image_io.rs:394-397`), so de-duplicate the identical views or cap at fit plus one native view.

**Targeted weak spot and metrics.** Ranking quality (AUROC, recall at fixed FPR) at no more than today's 1.7–1.9 views per image, or throughput at equal operating-point precision and recall. Text-dense images.

**Evidence.** On NTIRE hard validation, scoring all six views instead of early exit raised **clean recall at 1% FPR from 51.8% to 60.5%** (AUROC 0.938 → 0.955), on an oracle cutoff resting on about six false positives. Distorted AUROC barely moved (0.734 → 0.739). At the shipped cutoff, all views was slightly *worse* on distorted inputs: 263 against 272 generated images found, and 106 against 103 authentic ones flagged (`docs/EVIDENCE.md:224`). This is a clean-recall and speed item. TextureCrop's current version (v4) reports texture-selected crops beating centre crops by **+4.3 balanced accuracy, +6.9 AP and +6.1 AUC**, and beating resizing by +12.1 / +18.2 / +14.9. It uses a default of 10 crops (5 and 10 nearly equal), Global Histogram Entropy selection and logit averaging, all on clean images of at least 1024 px ([TextureCrop](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.15500v4)). SPAI's trained attention aggregator beat a ten-crop mean by 5.7 AUC (91.0 vs 85.3). That is a gap between rows of a table, against a trained module that post-hoc weights will not match. The same table has mean above max (85.3 vs 84.2) ([SPAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19417v2)). LaDeDa's average pooling beat max on accuracy on all three benchmarks (UFD 96.3 vs 88.9). On AP it won two of three, and max was 0.2 AP higher on ForenSynth ([LaDeDa](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09398v1)). Panoptic Patch Learning reports that masking a single patch cut UnivFD's detection (recall in its Fig. 3b) by 18.7 ± 4.1% on average, which motivates multiple views but does not measure view pooling ([Panoptic Patch Learning](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01396v3)). For text, TextFake reports no detector above 80% on text-rich images and names a "Text Density Curse" (abstract only) ([TextFake](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01050)). That lands on Tintype's gradient-ranked crops, which favour dense text. AIGDoc measures a milder AUC drop of more than 7 points on documents ([AIGDoc](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14352)), and TextRich covers GPT-Image-2 receipts and UI screenshots ([TextRich](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19259)). Fit-first against fit-last is an untested hypothesis on both sides. The NTIRE winners' 512 or 288 px inputs reflect their encoders' native sizes, not view order.

**Expected effect on Tintype (extrapolated).** Small clean-ranking gains or equal ranking at fewer views. Operating-point verdicts barely change, as the repo found for early exit. Some throughput gain on small images, proportional to their share of traffic. A measured README limit for text-rich images.

**Cost.** No retraining. The replay tool is low-medium work. A shipped pooling change touches at least six hard-coded means (`src/detector.rs`, `src/eval.rs`, `src/scan.rs`, `src/server.rs`). It also forces refits of all four profiles, the exit margins and the second-opinion band, and a decision on video, which reuses the photo views. A least-textured view needs a new `ViewSpec` variant and selection routine in `src/image_io.rs`.

**Risks.** Per-view weights overfit the data that judges them. Fit on development sets plus the calibration split, use batch 7 and NTIRE hard validation for selection, and reserve NTIRE public and a new batch for claims. Smooth regions are where learned-codec reals already draw 47–75% false positives, so a least-textured view could raise FPR. Accept it only if FPR on that subset does not rise. A new pooling must be calibrated the same way it is deployed.

**Cheapest first experiment.** Count the share of development and batch-7 images whose short side is under 224 px, and time the de-duplicated path. Run the replay tool's pooling table (AUROC, recall at the default FPR, mean views per image) on the existing local `*-views.tsv` files. Score TextFake with `--all-views` and compare text-dense crops against the fit view.

### 6. Measure video FPR per real collection and audit visible watermarks

**Concrete change.** Extend the existing per-source video FPR table, which already covers Kinetics-400 at 0.3%, MSVD at 0.0% and MSR-VTT at 0.7% (`docs/BENCHMARK.md:690-694`), to new real collections. Those include Pixabay (outside the Davodi cohort), more Pexels, drone and gimbal, stabilised phone, screen-recorded and broadcast footage, all scored at the shipped threshold. Test a **canonical re-encode**: pass every clip through one shared H.264 encode before scoring and compare per-collection FPR. Run a **visible-watermark audit**: overlay Sora, Veo and Kling marks on about 300 held-out real clips, and crop or inpaint the marks off generated test clips. Then add Pixabay and graded footage to the video negatives and calibration pool, with at least 100 clips per sub-collection so that they count under Balanced(100). That needs a Pixabay fetcher in `src/plans.rs` and `src/fetch.rs`, MP4 renditions only, a licence check, and de-duplication against the Davodi `inputs.tsv`. If the audit shows a watermark shortcut, add random logo overlays to both classes. WaveRep band replacement is an optional recall item for the next video fine-tune. Video sampling hardening is **removed** from this recommendation; see the rejected list.

**Targeted weak spot and metrics.** Video real FPR per collection (3/20 Pixabay clips flagged in the Davodi cohort). FPR under watermark spoofing and recall under watermark removal.

**Evidence.** Cakiroglu et al. found detectors learn their real corpus's identity. At a threshold flagging 5% of the training benchmark's reals, they flagged **15% or more of at least one other real collection**. Recalibrating per collection brought FPR back to about 4–7% as seed means (single runs 2–12%). For their linear readout of WaveRep's scores, measured on two collection pairs, TPR went 0.95 → 0.93 on generator sets that do not match. More usefully for a frame-level head like Tintype's, **one shared re-encode removed most over-flagging**: the WaveRep readout's 24–27% FPR on DVF fell to 6% or below after an H.264 re-encode ([Cakiroglu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31004v2)). Recalibrating on Pixabay fixes Pixabay but bounds nothing on unseen collections, which makes the per-collection audit the essential part. RobustSora found that removing or spoofing visible watermarks shifted accuracy by up to 9.4 points (mean 6.6), significant for 7 of 10 detectors. Sora 2's drop was **11–14 points**, and watermark-aware augmentation recovered 3–4 ([RobustSora](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10248)). Tintype trains on Sora, Veo 3.1 and Kling clips, and its texture-ranked crops can land on a logo corner. RA-Bench offers 17,886 content-matched crisis-event videos as a larger test than the 20-clip cohort, whose 95% interval for 3/20 is about 5–36% ([RA-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14391)). WaveRep's band replacement lifted balanced accuracy from 88.2 to 91.8 (AUC 97.6 → 98.7) for a single-generator model. That is recall-side evidence, and WaveRep itself flagged 4/20 Davodi reals ([WaveRep](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16802v2)). Frame-level scoring is robust to motion rebalancing (WaveRep 0.989 → 0.989), while motion-based D3 collapsed from 0.794 to 0.456 on one blurred frame ([Michels et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00948v2)). That supports keeping Tintype frame-based.

**Expected effect on Tintype (extrapolated).** Lower FPR on stock and graded footage, and a measured bound on collection-identity drift. A watermark shortcut, if present, would show as several points of FPR on spoofed reals. A small recall cost on stock-like generated clips is possible, because generators train on stock footage. Davodi's 20 reals cannot resolve the change, so the audit is the primary metric.

**Cost.** Data collection and a fetcher, then re-finalize-video, which moves the pre-registered 1.621 threshold. That requires a new `docs/PREREGISTRATION.md` entry and a fresh blind batch. The WaveRep augmentation is 1–2 days of new CPU code (DWT and band swap in `src/data.rs`) plus a full video retrain and freeze cycle.

**Risks.** The phase-5 fall in video FPR coincided with more generators and a stricter target as well as the cartoon and gameplay negatives, so the "negatives fixed it" story is confounded (`docs/BENCHMARK.md:735-740`). The Davodi result becomes in-sample unless its Pixabay assets are removed.

**Cheapest first experiment.** On macOS: `index-folder` on about 300 Pixabay clips and two other real collections, then `score-video --all-frames`, then `report` at the shipped threshold. Repeat after one canonical H.264 re-encode, and after overlaying Sora, Veo and Kling marks.

## Weight-space, refresh, face and speed work: recommendations 7–10

### 7. Interpolate toward Community Forensics before building any distillation

**Concrete change.** Three steps, each gated on the one before. **(i) WiSE-FT interpolation, no training.** A ten-line offline tool over the safetensors files computes θ(α) = (1−α)·θ_Tintype + α·θ_CF224 for α from 0 to 0.5. Both share the ViT-S/16 architecture. Run `finalize` for each α, then score GAN sets and batch 7 (development use only) at matched FPR. **(ii) Weight EMA** in `src/trainer.rs` as a default for future phases, a few lines with zero inference cost. **(iii) Logit distillation with replay**, only on stores Community Forensics did **not** train on (`tiny-genimage`, `stylegan3`, `tpdne`, and held-out GAN-like data). Cache CF-384 logits with `tintype score --reference commfor --all-views`. The cached logit comes from a clean 384 px view while the student sees augmented 224 px views, and the loader carries no item identity. So this needs a path→logit join in `spawn_loader`, a teacher placeholder and KD loss in `src/trainer.rs`, and flags in `src/train_cmd.rs` and `src/main.rs`. Make L2-SP toward CF-224 a secondary ablation: about ten lines next to the decoupled-decay term, with the init tensors `train_cmd` already loads. Drop face swaps as a target, because no training store contains them and FF++ is a blind test family.

**Targeted weak spot and metrics.** GAN recall (84–89%) at matched FPR. Batch-7 sets at or above CF on both precision and recall at matched calibrated FPR, against the second opinion's 26/30. Second-opinion throughput, for opt-in users only.

**Evidence.** WiSE-FT gained **4–6 points under distribution shift** at no training or inference cost ([WiSE-FT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01903)). Soups of sibling fine-tunes lie beyond the interpolation curve ([model soups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482)). In deepfake detection, plain weight averaging of specialists beat joint training cross-dataset, **0.834 vs 0.662 AUC**, and on unseen generators, 0.724 vs 0.665 ([R²M](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24367)). Continual deepfake-detection work supports distillation with replay over weight anchoring. CoReD distils from the previous model across GAN and face tasks ([CoReD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02408)). LiteUpdate found naive fine-tuning on 1,000 new-generator images cut NPR's SD 2.1 accuracy by 20.12 points ([LiteUpdate](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07192v1)). A search summary of the CAID benchmark reports EWC and LwF, which are L2-SP-like anchors, falling near chance while logit distillation with replay preserved old generators. Those figures are unverified ([CAID](https://scholar.hit.edu.cn/en/publications/benchmark-dataset-and-framework-for-continual-ai-generated-image-/)). Effort's often-quoted collapse is an effective rank (PCA components for 90% variance) falling from 479 to 238 under full fine-tuning, against 304 for LoRA and 316 for Effort, with mean accuracy 86.22 / 93.03 (LoRA, Table 8) / 95.19. That result is for CLIP ViT-L/14 trained on ProGAN only, so it applies to Tintype only by analogy ([Effort](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15633v4)). DGS-Net's +6.6% (per-benchmark gains 4.4 and 10.1) uses the frozen CLIP initialisation as teacher under narrow training ([DGS-Net](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13108)). GAPL describes aggregate performance stagnating, then degrading, as source diversity grows under a fixed data budget. It does not say older generators specifically degrade ([GAPL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12982)). Against the idea: B-Free's 95.2 vs 68.5 is end-to-end against a frozen linear probe on one generator, not anchored against unanchored training ([B-Free](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17671v2)). Huang et al.'s DINOv3 result compares against CF, which Tintype already beats about twofold on So-Fake-OOD, recall 0.84–0.86 against 0.40–0.42 ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22471); `docs/BENCHMARK.md:193, 287, 369`). Teacher signal may also be weak. Most of Tintype's GAN data is CF's own training set (commfor-small and commfor-small2), where CF-384's logits are near-saturated (`docs/DATA.md:63, 136-143`).

**Expected effect on Tintype (extrapolated).** If forgetting causes the GAN gap, a moderate α should recover part of it at some cost on modern generators. If not, the interpolation fails cleanly and saves the trainer work. Retiring stage 2 would restore 530–563 photos/s only for second-opinion users. The default is unaffected. Gap fill suggested this could rise to rank 5–6 if step (i) works.

**Cost.** Step (i): an afternoon plus `finalize` runs. Step (ii): trivial. Step (iii): a multi-file trainer change and a retrain, either a phase-6-only ablation (8k steps) or a full 36k-step lineage. Effort's SVD variant needs CPU-side SVD and is a larger task.

**Risks.** Pulling toward CF can give back Tintype's lead on modern generators (Imagen 3 recall CF 0.385 vs Tintype 0.922; So-Fake-OOD 0.421 vs 0.836). It can also inherit CF's false positives: 5.2% of batch-7 reals, and 107 of 300 reals on each of CRN and IMLE (`docs/BENCHMARK.md:180-230`). Guardrails are modern-generator recall and per-source FPR. Any new weights need re-finalize and a fresh pre-registered batch.

**Cheapest first experiment.** The interpolation sweep. Before step (iii), compute CF-384 logit entropy per GAN store from a cached score file, to see whether soft targets differ meaningfully from the hard labels.

### 8. Refresh in batches with weighted sampling, small replay and soups, and measure decay on held-out families

**Concrete change.** Add a **per-manifest or per-key mixing weight** (for example `--manifest path@w`) to `Sampler::new` in `src/data.rs`, `src/train_cmd.rs` and `src/main.rs`. Under √count weighting, a new generator with 500–3,000 images gets only about 0.2–0.5% of AI draws, so a 1–2k-step refresh shows it only about 40–150 images. Define a key-to-family map, which does not exist yet, and cap replay per family. A per-key cap of 50–200 barely trims the Community Forensics stores, which average about 54 images per key. Include in-the-wild and messenger-processed positives in each refresh. Batch refreshes, for example quarterly, behind one `finalize --weights <out>`, one refit of the photo second-opinion band, one early-exit check and one blind batch. `finalize` rebuilds the spec from `standard_spec()`, which **drops the second-opinion band** and resets early-exit margins (`src/eval.rs:485-501, 551-590`). Add `--also-trained-on` to `finalize-video` before any video refresh (`src/eval.rs:523-542`). Soup sibling refresh checkpoints instead of picking one. Measure decay first from the existing held-out-generator stages, not a new chronological retrain.

**Targeted weak spot and metrics.** Recall on newly released generators. Regression on older families (GAN, face, messenger). FPR after recalibration.

**Evidence.** Direct evidence on per-generator refresh sizes: Pantsios et al. used at most 305 images per generator (about 274 for training). Their **+9.14 (SPAI) and +8.01 (RINE)** average-accuracy gains came from fact-check-mined in-the-wild data plus generator data plus a 5% replay buffer (10% gave nothing more), measured against baselines already trained in the wild. They also found **generator-only data hurt in-the-wild robustness** ([Pantsios et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02567)). LiteUpdate fine-tuned on 500–1,000 images per new generator and showed naive fine-tuning forgets ([LiteUpdate](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07192v1)). DECODE reached 0.39% average forgetting over 8 sequential tasks, and 95.36% on 11 unseen generators, with 50 exemplars per class and closed-form head re-alignment on CLIP ViT-L/14 with LoRA. In its ablation, representation regularisation lowered forgetting more than head recalibration (0.81% vs 2.23% from a 3.30% baseline). `finalize`'s threshold refit is therefore not enough alone ([DECODE](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27882)). Replay is not automatically sufficient: DFIL (10.32%) and SUR-LID (40.61%) still forget heavily in DECODE's table. Vahdati et al. restored Sora AUC from 0.530 to 0.982 with under a minute of video, but never re-tested the original generators ([Vahdati et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15955v1)). FSD's peer-reviewed figure is +11.6% average accuracy on GenImage with 10 extra samples. Its arXiv v1 said +7.4% ([FSD](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wu25r.html)). AI-GenBench's protocol is a cumulative, expanding window, useful for chronological evaluation only ([AI-GenBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20865v3)). Unified models leave strong, corruption-robust traces ([Guess the Unified Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25254)), which suggests modest per-model samples suffice.

**Expected effect on Tintype (extrapolated).** Faster recovery on new generators with bounded forgetting, if the per-family regression gate holds. The headline metric, recall after the November 2026 cutoff, cannot be measured yet, because no post-cutoff generator exists.

**Cost.** Short GPU retrains on the Mac, plus sampler and finalize-video plumbing. Per-refresh protocol cost (finalize twice, band refit, blind batch) dominates GPU time, and fresh blind sources are running out (`docs/BENCHMARK.md:239-243`).

**Risks.** A generator-only refresh may degrade messenger recall, Tintype's weak spot. Replay is not automatically enough. Byte-only de-duplication risks near-duplicates of batch-7 content. Batch 7 is development data, and no confirm8 plan exists yet.

**Cheapest first experiment.** Re-run the phase-3 step from phase-2 weights with a small stratified buffer instead of full stores. Six generators were held out of phases 1–2 and measured unseen on So-Fake-OOD batches 1–2 (default recall 0.724 / 0.734), then phase 3 added 3.3k of their images with full replay (`docs/DEV_RESULTS.md:147-155, 243-251`). Compare per-generator recall on So-Fake-OOD batch 3 and per-family regression. Hold out the whole AR/unified family in the next chronological test.

### 9. Mix self-blended faces into training, behind a fresh face-swap hold-out

**Concrete change.** Generate SBI images offline from CelebA-HQ (7.5k aligned faces) and the FFHQ reals in commfor-small, using an external landmark tool, and record the store with digests in `docs/DATA.md`. Mix it into the **main head** at about 5% of fake draws, which needs recommendation 4's per-key mixture weight. Before training, add a fresh face-swap hold-out in `src/plans.rs`: the unused part of the CNNDetection deepfake folder, and the AIGIBench BlendFace, E4S, FaceSwap, InSwap and SimSwap archives. Fix the evaluation rule in `docs/PREREGISTRATION.md`. **Defer** the separate face head (multi-file engine, trainer and CLI work) and the face detector with a face-gated view. The engine has no conv2d kernels, and the FF++ benchmark images are already face crops (paths `deepfake/0_real/<video>_<frame>.png`, 50–72 KB), so a gated view cannot show a gain there. Widening scope to face swaps is a product decision (`README.md:510-512`).

**Targeted weak spot and metrics.** Face-swap recall (0.143 on FF++; CF 0.573). FPR on real faces (CelebA-HQ validation, So-Fake social reals, FF++ reals).

**Evidence.** SBI beat the prior unsupervised state of the art, **PCL+I2G**, by 4.90 points on DFDC and 11.78 on DFDCP. These are video-level AUCs with EfficientNet-B4 at 380 px. With the classifier held fixed, SBI data beat blending data, for example Xception on FF++ FaceSwap at 89.29 → 98.79. SBI's own limitations include 69.11% AUC on FFHQ vs StyleGAN and weakness on low-artifact deepfakes ([SBI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.08376)). In Effort's re-evaluation, SBI averaged **0.821 cross-dataset but 0.766 cross-method** on eight modern swappers, below UCF (0.824) and Effort (0.940), and scored 0.594 on FaceDancer and 0.701 on SimSwap ([Effort](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15633v4)). On DF40, SBI's 0.810 on FF-domain face swaps trails a CLIP model trained on FF reenactment at 0.932 ([DF40](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13495)). DeepfakeBench's standard pipeline feeds detectors dlib-aligned face crops ([DeepfakeBench](https://github.com/SCLBD/DeepfakeBench)). Deepfake-Eval-2024's in-the-wild drop is about 50% AUC for video detectors. Its 45% image figure concerns generic AI-image detectors (UFD, DistilDIRE, NPR), not face heads ([Deepfake-Eval-2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02857)).

**Expected effect on Tintype (extrapolated).** Gains concentrated on blending-style swaps (FF++ Deepfakes and FaceSwap, Celeb-DF). Little on modern non-blending swappers. Matching CF's 0.573 is not expected.

**Cost.** The mix-in is a short retrain plus an external landmark job. The separate head and face detector are weeks of work and are deferred.

**Risks.** SBI teaches "real face with a blending boundary = fake". Retouched, filtered or composited real photos, common in the messenger and social sets, could be flagged more often, and a rise in real-face FPR tightens the global threshold. Tuning against the 300/300 FF++ set spends its blind status.

**Cheapest first experiment.** Score 2,000 SBI blends of held-out real faces with the shipped weights. If Tintype already flags them, the training signal adds little. Then run a short mix-in phase and evaluate on the fresh hold-out, with real-face FPR guardrails, using `report --at` at matched FPR.

### 10. Profile the portable CPU path, simulate INT8, and write kernels only if both pass

**Concrete change.** Three gated steps on the Linux/Windows `portable-cpu` path. **(i) Profile** the share of wall time in the qkv, proj, fc1 and fc2 GEMMs against decode, resize, attention and element-wise work. The 35.2 photos/s figure is decode-to-verdict, with per-set rates of 18.9–40.1. **(ii) Fake-quant spike**, 1–3 days: an env var in `Lin::apply` in `src/cpu_vit.rs` round-trips weights (per-channel) and activations through int8. Collect activation statistics on development images under recommendation 1's augmentation. Then run `score --device cpu` with f32 and fake-quant on development manifests and diff the verdicts. Use twin-uniform or per-token scales for the post-GELU input to fc2, or keep that matmul in f32. As a cheap speed estimate, try `candle-core`'s Q8_0 quantized matmul, which is already a dependency (`src/weights.rs:3`). **(iii) Kernels**, 2–4 weeks and only if (i) and (ii) pass: new NEON SDOT/I8MM and AVX-VNNI microkernels with packing, runtime dispatch and an f32 fallback where VNNI is absent. The matmuls today go to Accelerate `cblas_sgemm` on macOS and the `gemm` 0.19 crate elsewhere, with no hand-written GEMM (`src/cpu_vit.rs:1-10`). Leave macOS alone. Accelerate runs on the AMX units, and the macOS default is the GPU.

**Targeted weak spot and metrics.** Linux/Windows CPU throughput (35.2 photos/s on 8-core aarch64). Verdict flips against CPU f32. Largest per-set recall change. Real FPR at the strict and sensitive operating points.

**Evidence.** PTQ4ViT's sub-0.5% ImageNet loss at W8A8 (ViT-S/224 81.39 → 81.00) needs twin-uniform quantizers and a Hessian-guided scale search. Its plain-uniform "Base PTQ", closer to the per-tensor scheme proposed here, lost **0.91 points on ViT-S/224 and 2.38 on ViT-S/224/32** ([PTQ4ViT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12293)). The only forensic quantisation study is quantisation-aware training with an NPR input and an added full-precision auxiliary path. Its Fig. 2, read approximately from the plot, shows naive 8-bit LSQ dropping unseen-generator accuracy from about 90.7 to 85.1 ([Tao et al.](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/59)). In the one study that tested it, calibrating INT8 on degraded images did not reliably improve robustness. INT8 object detectors were more noise-sensitive, but heavy JPEG cost under 2% at every precision ([arXiv 2508.19600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19600v1)). Quantisation can amplify small input perturbations ([Defensive Quantization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08444)). Tintype's token-merging test lost 6.2 and 14.0 recall points (`docs/BENCHMARK.md:911-916`), an analogy for how fragile forensic signal is under approximation. RepQ-ViT handles post-LayerNorm outliers but targets 4–6 bits ([RepQ-ViT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08254)).

**Expected effect on Tintype (extrapolated).** Bounded by Amdahl's law. If linear GEMMs are about 60% of Linux wall time and run 2.5× faster, end-to-end throughput rises about 1.55×, to roughly 55 photos/s, unmeasured. There is no macOS or GPU effect. Integer accumulation is exact, so a well-specified INT8 path could match bit for bit across aarch64 and x86, which f32 does not guarantee.

**Cost.** No training. The spike is low cost. Kernels are high cost, and three networks need separate calibration and gates: photo, video and CF-384.

**Risks.** The gate must be measured against fresh CPU f32 scores. GPU f16 and CPU f32 already disagree on 7 of 16,324 verdicts, about 4.3 per 10,000. The early-exit change shipped under a "pooled P/R within 0.001" rule while flipping 71 per 10,000 verdicts, so pooled agreement and a flip budget are different gates and both are needed. If only Linux gets INT8, the documented Linux/macOS verdict parity ends. Quantisation noise also changes which early-exit stage fires.

**Cheapest first experiment.** The profile, then the fake-quant spike on development data, reporting flips per stratum: clean, messenger, NTIRE noise.

## Where the evidence disagrees, and which side Tintype should take

**Codec false positives.** The draft read Cannas et al. as showing that codec false positives survive retraining. On CLIP-like detectors the problem is milder, 0.42–0.72 FPR against 0.96–0.99 for low-level CNNs ([Cannas et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03261v2)). Tintype's own NTIRE group shows a detector with no ranking there. The per-operation audit in recommendation 1 decides, not the literature.

**Paired consistency loss.** TeleAI and DCPT report gains. The top two NTIRE teams and DCPT's own B-Free comparison did better without one. Tintype should treat it as an ablation.

**Threshold transfer against ranking.** Note 08 called the NTIRE failure threshold transfer. The repo's experiment shows both are broken. Recommendations 1 and 3 are therefore paired, and calibration alone is not presented as a recall fix.

**Second-opinion value.** Seen pooled, the fusion adds almost nothing over a re-thresholded single network: same AUROC, 0.935 against 0.932 recall at 329 false positives. Seen per set, it doubles parity with CF (26/30 against 12–14/30). Recommendation 2 keeps the per-set objective and cuts the cost.

**Fit-view order.** Notes 01 and 07 disagree, and neither side has direct evidence. Only Tintype's own all-view logits can settle it.

**Colour augmentation and mixup.** SPAI measured chromatic augmentation hurting (91.0 → 80.5). NTIRE tests colour and tone distortions. In B-Free, mixup lowered balanced accuracy while raising AUC. Both go into separate ablation arms.

**PEFT and anchoring against full fine-tuning.** Effort and DGS-Net are narrow-data results. The broad-data counter-evidence compares frozen probes, not soft anchoring. The continual-learning literature (CoReD, LiteUpdate, DECODE) is closer to Tintype's situation and favours distillation with replay. The zero-cost WiSE-FT test should settle it before any trainer work.

## What verification changed from the draft

Recommendation 1 kept its rank but changed shape. It is now a cheap in-process curriculum, with codecs, WebP and AVIF moved to offline paired stores or ImageIO, and the paired loss demoted to a stop-gradient ablation. Messenger caps rose to 720 px, share cycles cover 1–6, and colour became a separate arm. NTIRE became an upper bound, the Cannas et al. framing was corrected, and pair ids, a split by source, re-finalize and a fresh blind batch were added. Recommendation 2, the second-opinion change, moved up from rank 3 and was rescoped from learned symmetric fusion to raising `lo`, backed by an archive sweep. Recommendation 3, calibration, moved down from rank 2 and became a diagnostic plus a pre-registered UNSURE, with the degradation head split off as a gated retraining project, RAW replaced by conformal-shift theory, and DACOM added. Recommendation 4 widened to all decoder families and open AR models. It gained an audit-first order, a written labelling policy with an NTIRE codec guardrail, paired batching, a sampler mixture weight and a contamination audit. Inpaints were deferred, and the B-Free, Aligned and DDA numbers were corrected. Recommendation 5 became a clean-ranking and speed item with a replay tool, a small-image de-duplication fix, TextureCrop v4 and a text-density check. Recommendation 6 dropped its sampling half, whose premise was false, and gained a canonical re-encode test and a watermark audit. Recommendation 7 now leads with zero-training WiSE-FT interpolation and EMA, restricts distillation to stores CF did not train on, and drops face swaps as a target. The refresh loop moved up from 10 to 8, with a sampler weight, finalize fixes and a leave-out replay experiment in place of a premature chronological test. SBI moved from 8 to 9 as a main-head mix-in only. INT8 moved from 9 to 10 with corrected cost and a Linux-only scope.

## Conclusion

Verification sharpened the main finding rather than overturning it. Tintype's distortion failure is real and largest. But the field's fix is plain augmentation at scale, not the clever pairing losses that drew attention in 2026 preprints. The most decision-relevant results came from Tintype's own archives, not the literature. The second-opinion sweep turned an hours-long "learned fusion" project into a four-number edit that cuts stage-2 work 4.6×. The Davodi frame logits showed that a whole video-hardening programme targeted a cause that was not there. Re-reading `transfer-diagnostics.txt` showed that no threshold can fix the neural-codec group, so the precision story there has to be abstention or retraining. Read twice, the score archives are the cheapest evidence Tintype owns.

The second implication concerns Tintype's evaluation protocol, which is now its binding constraint. Every recommendation that changes weights, and most that change calibration, spends a blind batch. Batch 7, NTIRE, Arena and Frontier are all seen, and fresh sources are thinning. Building a confirm8 plan with degraded variants made by procedures *not* used in training, before any retraining starts, is the precondition for believing any number this report predicts.
