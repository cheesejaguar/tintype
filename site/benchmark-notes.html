Benchmark notes

Scoring and sample coverage

Tintype scores every photo with its own network and asks the Community Forensics network for a second opinion when it is unsure, on about a quarter of photos. The table leaves out one set of 6,990 photos from a collection Tintype trains on; there it found 74.2% of AI photos and flagged 0.14% of real ones, and GPTTrace found 76.5% and flagged 0.03%.

Comparison with GPTTrace

Tintype’s and GPTTrace’s precision on batch 9 are not separated: the 95% interval for the difference runs from −0.09 to +0.40 points. GPTTrace’s recall is 19 points lower, and most of the gap is on older generators. GPTTrace finds more than Tintype on a few recent ones, including PhotoMaker and Midjourney. See the GPTTrace comparison ↗

Checked on a blind split

The table is now version 1 of the Tintype Photo Benchmark. Its blind split holds 23,843 files from the same 27 sets that nothing had used before, and it was registered before anything was scored. There Tintype scored 99.0% precision, 93.0% recall, 1.2% false positives and 92.5% recall at 1% false positives, all within 0.3 points of the table. See the benchmark ↗

Tests with earlier photo weights

The next three results were measured with the previous photo weights and have not been re-run.

73.1% vs. 23.7% recall at 1% false positives. Tintype and Community Forensics scored the same 2,038 images after both classes were re-encoded with the same JPEG settings. GPTTrace reached 76.7% on those JPEGs. See the external Arena comparison ↗

Matched-rate comparisons set the thresholds retrospectively on each batch. Default recall was 14.3% on the FaceForensics++ face-swap subset. On the distorted NTIRE 2026 public-test JPEGs, Tintype found 2.2% of generated images at 1% false positives. See the NTIRE comparison ↗ Use flags to prioritize review and corroborate them with other evidence. See the evidence and limitations ↗

94.8% recall, 0.35% of authentic photos flagged on 14,526 held-out originals that Tintype never trained on, at the shipped cutoff. On 4,800 copies put through JPEG, resizing, reposts, blur, noise and sharpening: 79.3% and 1.62%. Every authentic original there is a large camera JPEG and every generated one a small PNG, so the originals are the easy case. See the held-out split ↗

Interpreting flags

How often a flag is right depends on how common AI-generated files are in what you scan. If 1 in 100 is AI-generated, about 45% of default-profile flags are, at batch 9’s 93.3% recall and 1.2% false-positive rate. For GPTTrace, at 74.1% and 0.8%, about 49% are.
